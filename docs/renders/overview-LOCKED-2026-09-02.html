The horizon rule, applied everywhere

No money figure renders without its horizon named. Every card now shows to date and period total together, never one alone. Revenue, COGS and Gross margin all carry "period to date" on the hero.

Card sub-lines became a two-up grid: left is the to-date pair, right is the period pair. Same shape on all three cards, so the eye compares like with like.

"Actual" is now "Actual spent" in the P&L. "Budget to date" and "P9 budget" are used consistently and never interchangeably.

This is not cosmetic. Four money defects this month were the same mistake - a full-period number divided by a to-date number - and each was invisible because nothing said which was which.

Wording pass

Earned envelope is now Budget at this revenue - it is the cost your target percent buys at the revenue you actually made, and the sub-line says how much more or less that is than planned.
budget ÷ budget is now does not move with revenue - the operator-relevant fact rather than the formula.
Points behind is now Amount behind, with dollars leading and the percent as context.
Pace became Will the budget last - one number and one sentence. The two-cell row and the progress bar are gone.
To date reads period to date everywhere.

Percent is the KPI

Target percent is budgeted cost over budgeted revenue. It no longer divides by actual revenue, so it does not move when revenue moves. It is also horizon-independent - prorating both sides cancels, so the target is the same on day 1 and day 28.

What that fixed: on P8 the board showed COGS 60.9% vs a 60.9% target - 0.8% under. The true target is 56.0%, so the account was 4.1% over. Dividing the budget by a revenue shortfall had quietly lowered the bar to meet the result. Margin flips the same way: 39.9% against 44.0%, behind, not ahead.

Earned envelope is the new number. At the revenue actually made, the target percent buys $87,356 of cost - $7,726 less than the plan allowed, because revenue came in 8.1% short. That is the sentence an operator needs: revenue dropped, so your cost budget dropped with it.

Simplification pass

Two budget horizons appear only where they differ. On a closed period or a rolling window, budget to date is the period budget - showing both was noise. Those ranges now carry one budget column everywhere: cards, tables and the P&L.

The period budget has one home per surface. It was appearing three times on the cost side - card, lever table and Pace. The card carries the pair, the lever table judges against budget to date only, and Pace owns the period envelope because runway is the question the envelope answers.

Same information, roughly a third fewer figures on an open period and half as many on a closed one.
