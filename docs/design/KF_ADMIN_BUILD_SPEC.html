Interaction spec CC builds against

  • Keyboard. ↑↓ move through visible service rows across group boundaries, Enter opens the selected row in the rail, Esc clears the rail selection, / focuses search. The keyboard cursor is a distinct navy tint, visibly different from the green selected state - one is where you are, the other is what is open.
  • Unsaved-change guard. Editing the price field marks the rail dirty. Selecting another service, switching account, or clearing shows the guard in the rail - never a browser confirm dialog. Discard is destructive-coloured; Go back is the safe default and takes focus.
  • Save is disabled until the value parses, differs from the current price, and carries a reason. Disabled state explains itself in the field hint rather than being silently inert.
  • Motion. Rail content fades and rises 6px over 280ms on selection change; rows and buttons transition backgrounds over 120ms; the toast rises over 280ms. All of it zeroes under prefers-reduced-motion at the token layer.
  • Elevation is four tiers only: cards at e1, dropdowns at e2, the app shell at e3, popovers and the toast at e4. Nothing invents a fifth shadow.
  • Focus rings are visible everywhere - navy on light surfaces, green on the dark rail, always 2px with 2px offset. Every interactive element is a real button.
  • Toast is the shared SC toast from PR-K, bottom centre, pause on hover. Not a new pattern. Success dismisses at five seconds; failure holds for eight, because a message that offers a retry has to survive long enough to be read and acted on.
  • A failed save changes nothing and keeps everything. The entered value stays in the field, the rail stays dirty, and the toast says so explicitly - "nothing was changed and your entry is still here" - so the operator does not retype. Try again re-fires the same save.
  • Symmetry. The catalog column grid is fixed at 1fr / 104 / 108 / 96 so headers and every row share one grid. Card header and footer carry equal 15px side padding, so the cream bands line up top and bottom. The rail scrolls its content while its footer actions stay pinned, so Archive never drifts up the panel on a short service.

Three decisions inside this render worth your eye

  • Fee accounts get a different rail, not a disabled one. They have a real editor today. The rail shows the annual amount and its effective date, and per-service pricing never appears at all - which is the standing ruling that a fee account's billing is the fee, not its services.
  • The backdate warning names the money, not just the date span. "233 days will recompute, weeks already billed become $11.00, AP is emailed with the credit owed" is what the operator actually needs to know before pressing save. It is the same event that produced the $3,892 credit on TBR.
  • Archived services cannot be priced at all. The rail refuses and offers Reactivate instead, rather than letting someone set a price on something that is not running and wondering later why nothing changed.
