Entry ledger - good, better, best

Three escalating answers. All of them start from one question I could not get past: the chef is entering counts, so why is the largest number on the panel a dollar figure they cannot act on? Money still matters here, but as an error-catcher rather than a goal. Each direction takes that further than the last.

Good

Stop being a table. Column headers exist to disambiguate, and with one count and one dollar figure per line there is nothing to disambiguate. Kill the headers, kill the tildes, let type weight say what is entered and what is projected. Total moves to the bottom, where a receipt puts it.

What I changed and why

  • Killed the column headers. They were the misalignment you spotted, and they were never earning their line. One count and one dollar figure per row is self-evident.
  • Killed the tildes. Twelve repetitions of "approximately" is noise. Projected versus entered is now carried by weight and colour, which the eye reads without decoding.
  • Dropped the dollar signs from rows. Said once, at the total.
  • State by dot, not colour alone. Filled means entered, ring means pending, lit ring means you are here. Passes the colour-blind test and reads faster than text ever would.
  • Total moved to the bottom. A receipt totals at the end. It also puts the number next to the button that commits it.
  • The four dead rows fold. They were 40% of the height, saying nothing.
  • Variance flagging (Best only). A count far off its projection gets caught before save, not after billing. Typing 11 instead of 110 is the realistic mistake here, and it is invisible in a wall of numbers.

My recommendation

  • Best, and not for the polish. The variance check is the only thing in these three renders that prevents an error rather than presenting one more neatly. Everything else is craft; that is function.
  • The threshold needs a ruling from you - percentage off projection, or absolute count, and how far off before it speaks. It should be rare enough that people read it when it appears.
