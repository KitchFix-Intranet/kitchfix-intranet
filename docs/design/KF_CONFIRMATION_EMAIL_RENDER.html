Two design decisions I made — push back if either is wrong

  • No bank details, no pay button, no invoice number. The real invoice carries remit-to instructions and a "View and pay" link. This one deliberately does not. If an operator ever forwards it to a client by accident, a watermark alone is thin protection — a document with no way to pay it cannot be acted on. The trade is that it is not a perfect visual match for the invoice.
  • Generated by us, not pulled from QuickBooks. Pulling the QBO PDF would give the actual invoice document with all the payment apparatus and no way to watermark it. Generating our own lets us strip what should not be there and add what should — who approved it, and when.

    • One recipient change this needs

  • The RDO is not currently on the confirmation email. Kevin asked for "all account managers and RDO" — the managers are already covered by the salaried list, but Shane is on the chase escalation only. He needs adding to this one.
  • Everyone else is already right: the account's salaried staff, Sebastian, Kevin, Joe, Josh, and whoever finalized.

    • What is different from the current confirmation email

  • Intranet shell instead of the older card layout — matches the chase emails and the invoice notifications.
  • The PDF is new. Today the email links to QuickBooks, which most operators cannot open. The attachment is the first time a chef can see what was billed for their own week.
  • Names who approved it. The review gate means somebody signed off on every day — the email should say who.
