Bulk entry - good, better, best

The layout is pushed right and the nested cream card is inconsistent, but those are symptoms. The real problem is the shape: six accordions, each holding the same table, so reviewing a week means scrolling through six identical tables to find the one day that differs. Bulk entry exists precisely because the days are mostly the same. The design should exploit that, not fight it.

Good

Consistent accordion

Keep the shape, fix everything wrong with it. The left gutter goes so the content starts where the eye already is. Day names stop truncating. The nested cream card - a card inside a card inside a table - collapses to one surface. Rows use the same grid as the entry ledger, so the two surfaces finally look related. Zeros fold.

Better

Pattern, then exceptions

Say the values once. That is what bulk entry means. The pattern sits at the top as the thing being applied, and below it the days it applies to - each one a single line. A day that differs from the pattern is tinted and says how. You review six days in six lines instead of six tables.

Best

The week at once

Services down, days across. The whole week in one table, no expanding, no scrolling to compare. This is how anyone would do it in a spreadsheet, which is exactly the mental model a chef brings. Any cell that breaks the pattern is tinted - so the outlier finds you instead of you hunting for it. Totals sit at the foot of each column where they belong.

Why Best

  • Six accordions become eight rows. The entire week fits without scrolling or expanding anything.
  • The outlier is the point. Wednesday's Lunch at 60 is visible in the first second. In the current design you would have to expand all six days and compare tables from memory to find it.
  • It matches the mental model. A chef checking a week of counts is doing a spreadsheet task, and this is the spreadsheet shape.
  • No nested containers. One table on one surface, rather than a card holding a card holding a table.
  • Column totals do real work. Meals and dollars per day sit under their own column, so a wrong cell shows up twice - in the cell and in the foot.
  • Same discipline as the ledger redesign: no tildes, no per-cell dollar signs, tabular figures, zeros folded, and the outlier tint reuses the amber already used for needs-attention everywhere else.

Open question for you

  • Should the cells be editable in place? The matrix makes it natural - click Wednesday's Lunch, type 60, tab onward. That turns bulk entry from "apply a pattern then fix days one by one" into a single surface where the whole week is entered. It is more build than the read-only version, and it is a real question rather than an assumption, so I have rendered it read-only and left the decision with you.
  • Seven-day weeks add one column. At the modal's current width that fits, but it is the width worth checking on a 1024px laptop before committing.
