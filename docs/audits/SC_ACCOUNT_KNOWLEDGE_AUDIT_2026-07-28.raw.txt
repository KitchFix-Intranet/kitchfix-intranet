[chunk 1] section="The one-line model"
From: Money Model - Service Calendar Billing Mechanics (REF-140), Section: The one-line model

**Per-meal accounts:** the client's per-meal invoice equals `actual_count × post-SF invoice
rate`. Any Service Fee (SF) is billed separately as a flat annual amount on its own
schedule. On accounts where the SF discounts the per-meal rate (CIN-AZ, TXR-AZ,
TBR-FL MiLB), the post-SF invoice rate is `sticker × (1 - SF%)`. On accounts where the SF
does not discount per-meal (TBJ-FL) or where there's no SF at all (CIN-KY, TBJ-NY), the
invoice rate equals the sticker. On flat_fee accounts (CIN-OH, STL-MO, STL-FL, TXR-TX-H,
TXR-TX-V), the client is not per-meal-billed at all - the fee schedule is the money.
**The operator's KPI target** is per-meal invoice revenue plus service-fee revenue,
combined as two ADDITIVE lines per the P&L (2400.1 Meal Service (Home) + 2300 Service
Charges + 2200 Catering Revenue). It is NOT sticker × count. There is no "sticker
revenue" as a first-class computation anywhere in this model - sticker prices are a
reference number that lives in the workbooks for planning-tab arithmetic, not a lens the
KPI dashboard or app displays.

[chunk 3] section="Terminology (used consistently below)"
From: Money Model - Service Calendar Billing Mechanics (REF-140), Section: Terminology (used consistently below)

- **Sticker rate.** The workbook projection-tab price. Historical planning number. NOT the
  invoice. Reference-only.
- **Post-SF invoice rate.** The workbook actuals-tab price. What the client is billed
  per meal on the per-meal invoice line. For SF% accounts this equals `sticker × (1 -
  SF%)`; for flat-SF, no-SF, and flat_fee-tracking accounts, sticker equals post-SF
  (the two rates are the same number). This is the ONE per-meal-price concept the app
  displays.
  Historical doc language sometimes called this "cost basis" (see
  the SC working record line 33, older bible text, seed script comment
  `scripts/_seed_sc_from_xlsx.mjs:440-445`). That name is misleading - it's not COGS,
  it's the invoice line. Use "post-SF invoice rate" going forward.
- **Service Fee (SF).** A separate contracted amount billed alongside per-meal (SF%
  accounts and flat-SF accounts) or in place of per-meal (flat_fee accounts). Paid on
  its own schedule per contract; typically flat annual.
- **KPI / all-in revenue.** What operators are measured on: per-meal invoice + SF, as
  two additive P&L lines. This is NOT `sticker × count`; it's `post-SF invoice ×
  count + SF flat`. Both come from the same underlying `sticker × count` decomposition
  when you re-derive the SF portion, but the model never uses sticker directly - it
  uses the two additive contract-defined revenue lines.

[chunk 4] section="Per-topic model > (a) Projections - what a projected count and projected price mean"
From: Money Model - Service Calendar Billing Mechanics (REF-140), Section: Per-topic model > (a) Projections - what a projected count and projected price mean

**Plain:** A projection is a planning estimate for a day - "we think we'll serve 100 MiLB
lunches." It is not billing; it is budgeting. The price attached to a projection is the
**post-SF invoice rate** (`sc_service_prices` `price_kind = 'projected'` today - the name
of the column is legacy from before the alignment; the price it holds is the invoice
rate per Price Review v3). Projected revenue on the app equals `projected_count ×
post-SF invoice rate`, which is what the client would be per-meal-invoiced if the
projection came in exactly.
**Mechanism:**
- Sheet: workbook projection-tab row-2 price = sticker rate. Legacy artifact for
  building an annual budget forecast.
- PG: `sc_daily_projections(account_key, service_id, service_date, projected_count)`
  holds the counts. `sc_service_prices(service_id, price_kind='projected',
  effective_date, price)` holds the price valuing them. Since 2026-06-16, this price
  is the post-SF invoice rate (Price Review v3), not the sticker.
- View: `projected_revenue = projected_count × pr_proj.price` in
  `sc_daily_revenue` (sc-8b line 241).
- App: modal group header + tile rail + workspace strip all read this price.

[chunk 5] section="Per-topic model > (b) Actuals - what saving actuals means, which price values them"
From: Money Model - Service Calendar Billing Mechanics (REF-140), Section: Per-topic model > (b) Actuals - what saving actuals means, which price values them

**Plain:** When an operator saves 100 MiLB Lunch actuals, KitchFix served 100 meals that
day. The per-meal invoice line for that day equals `100 × post-SF invoice rate`. On
CIN-AZ MiLB that's `100 × $12.90 = $1,290`.
**Mechanism:**
- Sheet: workbook actuals-tab row-2 price = post-SF invoice rate. For SF% accounts
  this appears in the sheet as a literal `=sticker * factor` formula (CIN-AZ:
  `=29.00888*0.7`; TXR-AZ: `=35.72125*0.8`; TBR-FL MiLB: `=23.77*0.75`). For flat-SF
  / no-SF accounts it equals the projection-tab price.
- PG: `sc_daily_actuals(account_key, service_id, service_date, actual_count)` holds
  counts. Post-sc-8c (this migration), NO `'actual'`-kind rows exist in
  `sc_service_prices` - the view's `COALESCE(pr_act.price, pr_proj.price)` falls back
  to `pr_proj.price` (the post-SF invoice rate) for every account.
- View: `actual_revenue = actual_count × COALESCE(pr_act.price, pr_proj.price, 0)`
  in `sc_daily_revenue` (sc-8b line 242). With sc-8c applied, this equals
  `actual_count × post-SF invoice rate` for every account.
- App: DayDetail toast / aggregate / week card echo this from the view via the #361
  server-authoritative saved-totals path.

[chunk 8] section="Per-topic model > (e) The KPI / all-in lens"
From: Money Model - Service Calendar Billing Mechanics (REF-140), Section: Per-topic model > (e) The KPI / all-in lens

**Plain:** Operators are measured on total revenue "all-in with service fees." Per the
P&L that leadership reviews, revenue is three additive lines:
- **2400.1 Meal Service (Home)** = per-meal invoice = `actual_count × post-SF invoice
  rate`.
- **2300 Service Charges** = Service Fee attributable to the review period. For an SF%
  account this is the SF% component of the corresponding meal service (the flat-annual
  SF, prorated per period per operator convention). For flat-SF it's the SF flat
  prorated. For flat_fee it's the fee-schedule amount prorated to the period.
- **2200 Catering Revenue** = smaller, account-specific extras (some contracts have
  side revenue lines - see per-account digest in the SC working record).
  - **Named ancillary line (out of SC scope): CIN-AZ Owners Week Caterings + Fantasy Camp** = the CIN-AZ P&L 2200 Catering Revenue line (~$52,000/yr). Discrete special-event caterings, billed separately, NOT part of the recurring service-calendar meal model - excluded from the SC per-meal export. Contrast with TBR-FL's BGC, which IS in-scope SC revenue. Do not treat the two 2200 lines the same. [A-10 CONFIRMED, LEDGER §T]
**KPI total = 2400.1 + 2300 + 2200.**
**Not** `sticker × count`. There is no reason for the KPI to compute `sticker × count`
directly - the same figure lands via `2400.1 + 2300` because the SF and the per-meal
discount are two sides of the same contract mechanic. This is why:
- No `'sticker'` `price_kind` exists in `sc_service_prices` today.
- The KPI dashboard consumes `sc_daily_revenue.actual_revenue` (= 2400.1) plus
  `sc_fee_schedule` (= 2300) per the SC_KPI_PUSH_CONTRACT.
- The SC calendar UI does NOT show the KPI/all-in figure. It shows the per-meal
  invoice (2400.1). Operators mentally add the SF band; the KPI dashboard shows both
  additively.
If a future decision decides the SC calendar should ALSO show the all-in figure
on-screen, the implementation reads both `sc_daily_revenue` and `sc_fee_schedule` and
sums - no schema change needed.
**Citations:** the SC working record §5.1 lines 97-104; the SC working record
lines 17-29.

[chunk 13] section="Per-topic model > (j) Effective-dating - how price changes over time are represented"
From: Money Model - Service Calendar Billing Mechanics (REF-140), Section: Per-topic model > (j) Effective-dating - how price changes over time are represented

**Plain:** Price history is preserved. A CPI bump or contract renegotiation is a NEW
row in `sc_service_prices` with a later `effective_date` - the old row stays. Historic
invoices continue to price at whatever rate was effective on the service date.

now; a new escalated rate = a new `sc_service_prices` row, applied via the admin
price editor (PR #224 for fenced backdate mode). This is a GOTCHAS entry as of this
PR - if a future maintainer opens a workbook and sees the escalator formula, that's
historical, not operative.

[chunk 0] section="Purpose & How to Use"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: Purpose & How to Use

How KitchFix manages the financial side of the business - from the site P&L to company reporting.
> ANCHOR: Margin is made or lost on the site floor every day, and protected by clean reporting and controls behind it. This manual connects the two - what the Executive Chef controls daily, and how it rolls into the company's books.
This manual defines KitchFix's financial operations: the two contract models, how a site's P&L is owned and managed, cost discipline for food and labor, budgeting, invoicing and billing, reporting, and the controls that keep it all clean. It applies to everyone who touches site finances - Executive Chefs, RDOs, and the Accounting team.
> NOTE: [OPEN - FINANCE INPUT] The reporting pack structure, approval thresholds, and the invoicing/billing workflow detail below are framework placeholders to be confirmed and filled in with Accounting (Sebastian Castro) before this manual goes Live.

[chunk 1] section="The Two Financial Models"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: The Two Financial Models

> ANCHOR: This manual is the financial hub. It orients on how KitchFix money works and points down to the detail: the money-model mechanics live in REF-140 (Money Model), and each account's specifics live in its Account Record (REC-101 through REC-111) and Contract Digest. This hub defers to REF-140 for money-model mechanics and does not independently restate money-model figures; live prices resolve from the Price Book.
Every account runs on one of two models, and the model changes how the EC manages money.

[chunk 2] section="The Two Financial Models > Fee model"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: The Two Financial Models > Fee model

The client owns the food cost; KitchFix manages purchasing and operations and is paid a management fee. The food budget is the client's money - managed tightly, reported transparently. This is the better-margin model for KitchFix because food cost is a pass-through, not a risk.

[chunk 3] section="The Two Financial Models > Full-service model"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: The Two Financial Models > Full-service model

KitchFix buys the food and delivers the operation under a single all-inclusive fee. Food cost is KitchFix's risk - over-ordering or waste comes straight out of margin, so cost discipline matters even more.
> NOTE: Know your account's model before you order, schedule, or report. The same action - an extra case ordered "to be safe" - is a client conversation under fee and a margin hit under full-service.

[chunk 4] section="Site P&L Ownership"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: Site P&L Ownership

The Executive Chef owns the site's P&L - not as a finance exercise, but as daily operating discipline.
The GM/EC is accountable for the site's financial result: food cost, labor cost, and the revenue or fee terms of the account. The RDO oversees P&L performance across the region; Accounting maintains the books and reporting.
- Food cost - driven by ordering, production, portioning, waste, and storage (Section 04).
- Labor cost - driven by scheduling to the business and to target (Section 05).
- Fee / revenue - governed by the account's SLA and model (Section 02).
> ANCHOR: Owning the P&L means knowing your numbers before someone asks for them. An EC who can't say where food and labor cost stand this period isn't yet running the site's finances - they're reacting to them.

[chunk 5] section="Food Cost Management"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: Food Cost Management

Buy what you need, use what you buy, and lose nothing to waste or spoilage.
- Order to par and forecast - order to the menu, the production plan, and forecasted covers - not habit (PB-010).
- Portion to spec - standardized recipes and portions keep food cost predictable plate to plate.
- Control waste - track waste honestly; it's the fastest signal of over-ordering, over-production, or rotation failure.
- Store & rotate - FIFO and correct storage so product isn't lost to spoilage.
- Inventory - count on the account's cadence; inventory value is part of the cost picture.
> NOTE: Under full-service, every one of these is margin. Under fee, every one is the client's trust. Either way, the discipline is the same.

[chunk 6] section="Labor Cost Management"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: Labor Cost Management

The right coverage to hit the standard, scheduled to the target - with accurate time, always.
- Schedule to the business - build the Rippling schedule to forecast and the labor target - enough to hit the standard, no more.
- Control overtime - overtime is approved in advance; worked overtime is always paid (POL-008). Unplanned OT is a scheduling signal to fix.
- Accurate time - every hour recorded; no off-the-clock work, ever (POL-008).
- Seasonal scaling - staff to the season - the workforce swings with the baseball calendar (POL-013).
> NOTE: Labor is the lever the EC controls most directly in the moment. The schedule is a financial document as much as an operational one.

[chunk 7] section="Budgeting & Forecasting"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: Budgeting & Forecasting

Plan the numbers before the period, so you're managing to a target instead of explaining a surprise.
- The budget - each account operates to a budget set with the RDO and Accounting - food, labor, and the fee/revenue terms.
- Forecasting covers - forecast covers from the schedule (homestands, road trips, events) to drive ordering and staffing.
- Re-forecast - update the forecast as the schedule changes; a stale forecast drives bad orders and schedules.

[chunk 8] section="Invoicing & Accounts Payable"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: Invoicing & Accounts Payable

Pay for what we received, verified and approved - no exceptions.
1. Verify against receiving - match every vendor invoice to what was actually received (PB-010, Section receiving).
2. Flag discrepancies - short shipments, price changes, and quality rejections are documented and resolved with the vendor.
3. Approve - invoices are approved per the approval matrix before payment.
4. Submit to Accounting - approved invoices route to Accounting on the account's cadence.
> NOTE: [OPEN - FINANCE INPUT] Approval thresholds, the AP workflow, and Accounting hand-off detail to be confirmed with Sebastian and filled in.

[chunk 9] section="Client Billing & Receivables"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: Client Billing & Receivables

Bill the client correctly and on time, per the model and the SLA.
- Fee accounts - invoice the management fee and any pass-through food cost per the SLA terms.
- Full-service accounts - invoice the all-inclusive fee per the SLA cadence.
- Backup & transparency - provide the documentation the SLA requires - fee accounts especially run on transparent food-cost reporting.
- Receivables - Accounting tracks receivables; the RDO supports resolution of any client billing question.

[chunk 10] section="Financial Reporting"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: Financial Reporting

Numbers the RDO and SLT can act on - on a rhythm, in a consistent format.
Each account reports its financial performance on a regular cadence - food cost, labor cost, fee/revenue, and variance to budget - rolled from the site to the region to the company. Reporting is restricted: financials are shared only with those who need them, by site, with sensitive lines (such as individual pay) removed before any team-level sharing.

[chunk 11] section="Controls & Approvals"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: Controls & Approvals

The basics that keep the money clean: approvals, separation, and a record.
- Approval matrix - spending and invoices are approved at the right level - not by whoever is closest.
- Separation of duties - the person who orders is not the only person who approves and reconciles.
- Documentation - every transaction has backup - the order, the receiving, the invoice, the approval.
- Restricted access - financial detail is shared on a need-to-know basis, by site, with sensitive lines removed.

[chunk 12] section="Variance Management & Escalation"
From: Financial Operations Manual (PB-009), Section: Variance Management & Escalation

Catch the variance early, explain it, and fix it - don't let it compound.
A variance to budget is information, not a failure - as long as it's surfaced early. The EC reviews food and labor against target on the reporting cadence and flags variances to the RDO before they become the period's story.
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Food cost over target | Review ordering, production, waste, and portioning; correct and report the cause. |
| Labor cost over target | Review the schedule against covers; adjust coverage to the business. |
| Billing or invoice dispute | Document, resolve with the vendor or client, and loop in Accounting and the RDO. |
| Material or repeated variance | Escalate to the RDO and Accounting early - with the cause, not just the number. |
> ANCHOR: A small variance explained now beats a large one discovered later. The RDO would always rather hear it early.
