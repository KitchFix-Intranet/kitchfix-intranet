## 3. Method

Three separate database queries, all filtered `fiscal_year = 2026`:

1. **Hourly budget:** `kpi_budgets` where `line_code = '3100.1'` and `account_key IN (11 accounts)`. Summed by (account, period_no).
2. **Salary budget:** `kpi_budgets` where `line_code = '3100.2'`. Same shape.
3. **Hourly actuals:** `labor_actuals_latest` `amount` column, filtered `fiscal_year = 2026`, aggregated by (account_key, period_no). `period_no` is a column on the row so no client-side derivation.
4. **Salary actuals:** `labor_salary_actuals` `amount`, filtered `week_start` in FY2026 (2025-12-29 through 2026-12-27). No `period_no` on this table; derived client-side via `periodOf(week_start)` from `src/app/kpi/labor/lib/periods.js`.

**Classification tolerance:**

- **RECON** = `|delta| <= $1.00` (absorbs rounding: P&L Actual columns round to whole dollars, DB carries cents; a $0.02 delta on a salary annualisation is arithmetic parity, not a variance)
- **MIDBAND** = `$1.00 < |delta| <= $100 AND <= 1%`
- **SIGNIFICANT** = `|delta| > $100 OR > 1%` (Kevin's variance bar)

**Null handling:** both sides null OR one side null + the other `|v| <= $1` = RECON. One side null + the other > $1 = SIGNIFICANT.

**Deliberately out of scope:** the API / rendering layer. Kevin's spec says "check second, and only for accounts where the DB reconciles" - this pass verifies DB vs finance only. If a rendering defect exists on top of a reconciled DB, it needs a separate on-prod inspection pass and is noted at the end.

## 7. Patterns

Two patterns account for most of the variance.

### Pattern A - DB has no hourly labor for P1-P4 on 8 of 11 accounts

**Every non-null-hourly account shows null in DB and a real number in P&L for P1 through P4** (except CIN-OH P5, which is the boundary week). The daily-detail floor is 2026-04-20 (start of P5 / end of P4), and the hourly-actuals pipeline attributes real dollars back from that floor forward.

### Pattern C - salary annualization drifts on high-turnover / active-hiring accounts

**STL - FL, STL - MO, TXR - AZ, TXR - TX - H** all show consistent salary variance across most periods. Two subpatterns:

1. **STL - MO** has a `-$384.58` variance in **six of eight** periods (P2, P3, P4, P5, P7, P8), with a compensating `+$4,615.42` in P6. This is a $5,000 timing shift - P6 caught up on approvals that finance had booked in earlier periods.
2. **STL - FL** shows a `-$6,000 / -$7,000` band across P3-P6, then reconciles P7 (+$357) and P8 (-$1,060) as the DB catches up.
3. **TXR - AZ** shows `-$6,500` variance in P1-P4 that decays to `-$0.01` by P8. Classic annualization convergence: DB starts on a stale annual-comp allocation and the P&L catches actual bumps period-by-period.

The **DB salary values are static within a run** (CIN - AZ shows `17692.32` for P1-P3, `15000.02` for P4, `12307.72` for P5-P8), consistent with `labor_salary_actuals.amount` being derived from `annual_comp_at_time / 26` per pay period. **P&L salary values move period-by-period** as actual pay posts. The two don't converge until the annualization stabilises.

**Verdict:** not the board being wrong, not the P&L being wrong. It's two different accounting bases for salary. Worth naming to a site leader who asks "why does the salary line drift?"

### Pattern D - CIN - KY salary P1-P3 shows $6,615 DB against $0 or $3,307 P&L

CIN - KY carries $6,614.92 salary every period in the DB (constant annualization). P&L shows `0` for P1-P2 and `3,307.46` for P3. The site leader was hired mid-year; DB annualization back-fills P1 as if the salary applied all year. P&L records only what actually posted.

Same underlying cause as Pattern C, more extreme because CIN - KY is a salary-only site (no hourly labor at all - see Pattern E).

**Verified.** Both accounts show `null` in DB hourly and either `null` or `0` in P&L hourly across all 8 periods. Kevin's expectation that these are salary-only sites is confirmed.

## 8. Lag hypothesis - is P&L on work-date or check-date?

- **native:** weeks whose Monday sits in `[periodStart, periodEnd]`
