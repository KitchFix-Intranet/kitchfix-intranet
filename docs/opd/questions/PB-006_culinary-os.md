## Module 1: culinary-os-philosophy - three checks

### Q1 - `culinary-certifications`

- **section_anchor:** `Responsible in the Kitchen`
- **prompt:** Which ServSafe certification does a **cook** hold, and which does a **salaried leader** hold?
- **options:**
  - **A.** Cooks hold Food Handlers and Allergens. Salaried leaders hold Food Manager and Allergens.
  - **B.** Everyone holds Food Manager and Allergens.
- **correct:** A
- **wrong:** The requirement is tiered. **Every cook holds ServSafe Food Handlers and Allergens. Every salaried leader holds ServSafe Food Manager and Allergens.** Certifications are kept current and on file - being out of date is the same as not having it.
- **right:** Correct. Food Handlers and Allergens for cooks, Food Manager and Allergens for salaried leaders, all kept current and on file.

---

### Q2 - `culinary-authentic-not-recipe`

- **section_anchor:** `Authentic in the Kitchen`
- **prompt:** A cook wants to put their family's version of a dish on the line. It is not the textbook traditional preparation. What does the handbook say?
- **options:**
  - **A.** Serve the traditional version - authenticity means fidelity to the original.
  - **B.** Cook it their way, well and with pride. Authenticity is not recipe fidelity.
- **correct:** B
- **wrong:** The section closes this directly: *authenticity does not mean every dish traces back to a traditional recipe - it means we do not pretend, and it means we care.* Where our team has roots, they cook those roots without apology, and pride is what the player tastes.
- **right:** Care and honesty make a dish authentic, not recipe fidelity. A cook respected for their background cooks that background with pride.

---

### Q3 - `culinary-authentic-justice`

- **section_anchor:** `Authentic in the Kitchen`
- **prompt:** You want to add a dish, but nobody on the line knows that cuisine well. What do you do?
- **options:**
  - **A.** Serve a simplified version and improve it over time.
  - **B.** Do not serve it yet. We earn the right to put it on the line.
- **correct:** B
- **wrong:** *If we cannot do a dish justice, we do not serve it* - we respect the cuisine, learn the recipes, educate our palates, and earn the right to put it on the line. Serving a lesser version is exactly the pretending the section rules out.
- **right:** Learn it first, then earn it onto the line.

## Module 2: culinary-os-standards - eight checks

### Q4 - `culinary-protein-tiers`

- **section_anchor:** `3.3 Protein Framework`
- **prompt:** Which protein tier is present at **every** meal period?
- **options:**
  - **A.** Lean.
  - **B.** Whichever tier the SLA specifies for that account.
- **correct:** A
- **wrong:** The SLA governs cadence for cured, processed, heavy, and high-end proteins - but not this. **Lean leads the menu** and is present at every meal period, at every account. Heavy is reserved primarily for arrival or post-game; high-end is budget and SLA dependent.
- **right:** Lean is the primary protein direction at every meal period. Heavy and high-end are the ones that vary by account and service.

---

### Q5 - `culinary-prohibited-oils`

- **section_anchor:** `3.5 Restricted and Prohibited Products`
- **prompt:** A delivery arrives with canola oil. What is it?
- **options:**
  - **A.** Restricted - usable if the SLA clears it.
  - **B.** Prohibited - never used in our operations.
- **correct:** B
- **wrong:** This is the distinction that section draws, and it matters. **Restricted** items can be used when the SLA clarifies it. **Prohibited** items are never used, full stop. Seed oils - grapeseed, canola, sunflower, soy, and any blend - are prohibited, as are nut oils.
- **right:** Prohibited. Seed oils and nut oils are never used in our operations, and no SLA clears them.

---

### Q6 - `culinary-soy-sauce`

- **section_anchor:** `3.5 Restricted and Prohibited Products`
- **prompt:** Soy sauce appears on both lists. What is actually allowed?
- **options:**
  - **A.** A retail bottle on the sauce station is fine; as an ingredient in buffet production, use tamari or coconut aminos.
  - **B.** It is prohibited outright - use tamari or coconut aminos everywhere.
- **correct:** A
- **wrong:** Close, and the split is the point. Soy sauce is **restricted** as a retail bottle on the sauce station, or inside pre-made sauces with transparent allergen labeling. It is **prohibited** as an ingredient for buffet production - that is where tamari or coconut aminos are used instead.
- **right:** That is the split. Retail bottle on the sauce station is restricted-but-permitted. As a production ingredient on the buffet, it is prohibited.

### Q7 - `culinary-menu-approval-order`

- **section_anchor:** `3.11 Menu Cycle and Approval`
- **prompt:** You have built next cycle's menu. Who sees it before the client does?
- **options:**
  - **A.** The Director of Culinary reviews it, you adjust, then it goes to the client.
  - **B.** Submit to the client for feedback first, then bring their input to the Director of Culinary.
- **correct:** A
- **wrong:** That inverts the workflow. Site Leader builds, **Director of Culinary reviews**, Site Leader adjusts from that feedback, **then** it goes to the client. The client sees a menu that has already been reviewed, not a draft.
- **right:** Site Leader builds, Director of Culinary reviews, you adjust, then the client sees it.

### Q8 - `culinary-allergen-free`

- **section_anchor:** `3.13 Allergens`
- **prompt:** A player asks whether the salad station is allergen-free. What do you say?
- **options:**
  - **A.** Confirm it if the station genuinely has no Top 9 present that day.
  - **B.** We never represent any meal or station as allergen-free.
- **correct:** B
- **wrong:** The standing position is absolute: **we do not represent any meal or station as allergen-free, ever.** Severe allergy accommodation is handled case by case at the Chef level through the dietitian - never by declaring a station safe.
- **right:** Never. No meal, no station, no exceptions. Severe allergy accommodation runs through the Chef and the team dietitian, individually.

### Q9 - `culinary-nuts-buffet`

- **section_anchor:** `3.13 Allergens`
- **prompt:** Where can peanuts and tree nuts appear in the operation?
- **options:**
  - **A.** Individually packaged and clearly labeled only - never on the open buffet.
  - **B.** On the buffet if labeled at the station in English and Spanish.
- **correct:** A
- **wrong:** Labeling is not the exception. **No peanuts or tree nuts on the open buffet**, at all. The only permitted form is individually contained and labeled - smoothies, bites, PB&J - per the Allergen Playbook. Nut oils are prohibited entirely.
- **right:** Individually packaged and labeled only. The open buffet never carries them, regardless of labeling.

---

### Q10 - `culinary-allergen-override`

- **section_anchor:** `3.13 Allergens`
- **prompt:** A client asks for an account-level exception to an allergen standard. What has to happen?
- **options:**
  - **A.** The Site Leader can approve it if the client accepts the risk in writing.
  - **B.** It must be documented in the SLA with rationale, an approval trail, and Director of Culinary sign-off.
- **correct:** B
- **wrong:** No account-level overrides exist without that chain. The document names all three parts - **documented in the SLA, with rationale, an approval trail, and Director of Culinary sign-off.** A client accepting risk is not the mechanism, and the Site Leader is not the approver.
- **right:** SLA documentation, rationale, approval trail, and Director of Culinary sign-off. Nothing less, and no verbal exceptions.

---

### Q11 - `culinary-vendor-substitution`

- **section_anchor:** `3.13 Allergens`
- **prompt:** A vendor substitutes a product mid-week. What has to happen before it goes on the line?
- **options:**
  - **A.** Check it against the original product's allergen profile, and notify the dietitian if it introduces a new Top 9 allergen.
  - **B.** Relabel the station to match the new product and carry on.
- **correct:** A
- **wrong:** Relabeling is necessary but not sufficient. Substitutions are **evaluated against the original product's allergen profile before service**, and **new Top 9 allergens require dietitian notification.** A substitution that quietly adds an allergen is how a labeled line becomes a wrong line.
- **right:** Evaluate against the original allergen profile first, and notify the dietitian if it brings in a new Top 9.
