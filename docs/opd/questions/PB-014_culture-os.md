## Q1 - `hospitality-player-scenario`

- **section_anchor:** `Hospitality: What We Mean`
- **prompt:** A player has had three bad at-bats and comes through the line without making eye contact. Which response is hospitality as this section defines it?
- **options:**
  - **A.** Give him the same warm greeting you give everyone, delivered exactly the same way every time.
  - **B.** Notice where he is, and adjust - a quieter greeting, or none, and make sure the thing he needs is already there.
- **correct:** B
- **wrong:** Close, but that is the version the section calls **performed**. A greeting delivered identically to everyone is a script, and the section is explicit that scripted hospitality is a checklist dressed up as caring. The anchor line is the test: *genuine cannot be performed, it has to be inherent.*
- **right:** That is it. The section defines hospitality as anticipating what the people around you need and acting on it - which means reading the person in front of you rather than running the same play regardless.

## Q2 - `hospitality-service-vs-hospitality`

- **section_anchor:** `Hospitality: What We Mean`
- **prompt:** The section separates service from hospitality. Which is true?
- **options:**
  - **A.** Service and hospitality are two names for the same standard, measured differently.
  - **B.** Service is the foundation. Once the guest needs for nothing, hospitality becomes possible.
- **correct:** B
- **wrong:** Not quite, and the distinction is the point of the section. **Service is the table. Hospitality is why they come back.** Service is anticipation - food ready, line clear, labels accurate. It is the floor. Hospitality is what becomes possible once service is right and the guest can actually be present.
- **right:** Right - and the order matters. Without service there is no hospitality to offer, which is why we do not trade the first for the second on a hard day.

## Q3 - `mission-genuine-operative`

- **section_anchor:** `Mission: Why We Exist`
- **prompt:** The Mission section names one word as the operative one. Which of these fails the Mission as written?
- **options:**
  - **A.** A line that runs perfectly, on time, correct every day, delivered with practiced professional warmth.
  - **B.** A line that runs perfectly and where the team genuinely knows the people coming through it.
- **correct:** A
- **wrong:** That is a well-run service operation, and it is not the bar. The Mission names **genuine** as the operative word, and lists what does not count: performed, scripted, transactional. The section says players, coaches, dietitians, training staff and our own teams should all be able to **feel the difference between a service operation and a hospitality operation**. Practiced warmth is the thing they can feel the absence in.
- **right:** Yes. Genuine is the operative word, and the test is whether the people we serve can feel the difference between a service operation and a hospitality operation.

## Q4 - `vision-partner-standard`

- **section_anchor:** `Vision: Where We Are Going`
- **prompt:** "Vital partner" is the Vision. Who sets that bar?
- **options:**
  - **A.** The client sets it through the Contract, and we meet what we have agreed to.
  - **B.** We hold ourselves to it before anyone holds us to it.
- **correct:** B
- **wrong:** The Contract is what we are obligated to deliver, but it is not where the bar sits. The section is explicit: vital partner **is the standard you hold yourself to before anyone holds it for you.** An operator the client has to hold to the standard is not a vital partner - they are a vendor being managed.
- **right:** Exactly. It is the standard you hold before anyone holds it for you, which is what makes the difference between being managed and being irreplaceable.

## Q5 - `values-humility-order`

- **section_anchor:** `Values: What We Believe`
- **prompt:** Something breaks at your site. The Humility value names an order for what happens next. What is it?
- **options:**
  - **A.** Understand it, explain what happened, then fix it.
  - **B.** Fix it, own it, then understand it.
- **correct:** B
- **wrong:** Close, and the order is the whole point. The section says the humble move is to **fix it, own it, and understand it - in that order.** Understanding matters - it is the third step, not the missing one. What the value warns against is reaching for it first, because an explanation offered before the problem is fixed and owned lands as an excuse, whatever it was meant to be.
- **right:** That is the order, and the section is deliberate about it. Fix it, own it, understand it. The explanation still comes - it just comes last, once it is an explanation and not an excuse.

## Q6 - `values-equity-respect`

- **section_anchor:** `Values: What We Believe`
- **prompt:** The Equity value says our hourly teams are treated with the same respect as our executive team. Which is closer to what that asks of you?
- **options:**
  - **A.** The same respect and the same hearing for everyone, whatever their role.
  - **B.** The same rules applied identically to everyone, so nobody can claim different treatment.
- **correct:** A
- **wrong:** That is consistency, and it is not the same thing. The value is about **respect and being heard**, not identical rules. The section's examples are the tell: paid sick time before the law required it, 1.5x when we ask hourly teams to work holidays. Those are not one rule applied evenly - they are choices about looking after people.
- **right:** Yes. Hourly teams treated with the same respect as the executive team, and every voice heard - the value is about respect and access, not identical rules.

## Q7 - `promise-third-genuine`

- **section_anchor:** `The Hospitality Promise: Best Food, Best Service, Best Hospitality`
- **prompt:** "Best Food, Best Service, Best Hospitality" is three things. What is the third one asking for?
- **options:**
  - **A.** A consistently high standard of guest interaction, delivered the same way at every site.
  - **B.** Genuine attention to the actual person - names, noticing, the small thing made into a moment.
- **correct:** B
- **wrong:** Consistency is Best Service. The third promise is the one that cannot be standardised: **genuine hospitality, not performed, not scripted, not transactional.** The section's examples are all specific to a person - learning a player's name and saying it, noticing a hard day and asking a real question, making a birthday or a welcome-back after a road trip into a moment.
- **right:** That is it. The third promise is the one that cannot be scripted, which is why the examples are all about a specific person rather than a standard interaction.

## Q8 - `standard-run-toward-problems`

- **section_anchor:** `The Vital Partner Standard`
- **prompt:** Something goes wrong at your site and it will affect the client. When does the client hear about it?
- **options:**
  - **A.** As soon as you have a fix, so you can bring the problem and the solution together.
  - **B.** From us first, before they find it themselves - even if the fix is not ready yet.
- **correct:** B
- **wrong:** Reasonable instinct, but there is a better answer here. The standard says **we run toward problems, not away from them**, and specifically that *the client hears it from us first, not the other way around.* Waiting for a fix risks them finding it first, and at that point the conversation is about trust rather than the problem.
- **right:** Exactly. Telling them first is what earns the partnership - being the kind of operator they never have to chase.
