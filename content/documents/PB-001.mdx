# About & How to Use

How this Handbook is built and how to use it.

## About This Handbook

The Leadership OS Handbook is the operating constitution of KitchFix Performance Food Service - the document every leader at every level reads and operates against. It defines the leadership roles that run the operation, the standards every leader is held to, the system that holds people accountable to those standards, and the relationship between the company, its people, and its clients.

It is not a personnel handbook. Personnel matters live in the corporate handbook. The Leadership OS Handbook is operating doctrine - what the company stands for, what every leadership role owns, and how the company holds itself together as it grows.

## How to Use This Playbook

Read it cover to cover once. Then keep it on your desktop and treat it as a reference. The Table of Contents lets you jump to the section you need.

Use it for onboarding, role definition, performance review preparation, and as the answer to "what is the standard?" when standards are in question.

> ANCHOR: This Handbook is the constitution. The Site Leader Scorecard, RDO Scorecard, Cadence Matrix, SLAs, and operational templates are the working tools. The Handbook frames the system. The tools run the system.

# Culture

Who we are. Why we exist. What we believe. How we show up. The foundation every role in this Handbook rests on.

## Why Culture is First

Before understanding your role as a leader at KitchFix, you need to understand the foundations of KitchFix and what we stand for.

Culture is our foundation. The Hospitality Promise, the Values, the Pillars, and the daily commitments are what make a Site Leader, an RDO, a Sous Chef, a Hospitality Manager, or a Corporate Field Chef act in a way that is recognizably KitchFix - across accounts, across leagues, across the season.

Without them, the role definitions are job descriptions. With them, the role definitions are how we live the culture in a specific seat.

## Origin - Founded on Hospitality

In 2010, Chef Josh Katt became a personal chef in Chicago, cooking for a client recovering from cancer. The work taught him something the restaurant world rarely does: the depth of relationship that forms when you cook for someone whose health depends on what is on the plate.

That experience set the foundation. Cook for the person, not the plate. Understand the body, the goals, the constraints, and let the food serve all three. Hospitality is not a feature of the meal. Hospitality is the meal.

In 2012, KitchFix launched as a Chicago-based meal delivery business built on that idea. The work caught on. Prominent organizations and winning teams started asking for what we do. Performance Food Service emerged as the practice we built around their needs.

The shape of the work changed. The standard did not. We are not a contract food service company that happens to work in sports. We are a hospitality company that built a performance food service practice - and that distinction shapes every decision we make.

## Mission - Why We Exist

We believe in the transformative power of genuine hospitality to instill confidence, enhance performance, and ensure every partner feels cared for, and poised to excel.

Genuine is the operative word. Performed hospitality, scripted hospitality, transactional hospitality - none of those are what we do. Players, coaches, dietitians, Athletic Training, Strength and Conditioning, front-office staff, and our own teams should all be able to feel the difference between a service operation and a hospitality operation. That is the bar.

## Vision - Where We Are Going

Be the vital partner for performance-focused organizations who deserve it all - an inspired variety of nutrient-rich, chef-crafted menus that provide wellness benefits while thrilling taste buds, and relentless client-centered service upholding the highest standards of hospitality.

Vital partner is a high bar. It is the standard you hold yourself to before anyone holds it for you. Every meal period, every conversation, every walk through the dining room is a chance to be irreplaceable to our client and those we serve.

## Values - What We Believe

Four values. They are how we hire, how we coach, how we promote.

- **Humility** - We are a company of humble individuals who support each other to get better every day. Senior people stay teachable. Junior people are heard. Egos do not run our kitchens.
- **Intentionality** - We grow with intention, passion, and creativity. Decisions are made on purpose. Menus are designed, not assembled. Hires are deliberate. Standards are written down so that every operator can hold them.
- **Sustainability** - We maintain sustainable growth and profit. Service that burns out the team is not sustainable. Pricing that erodes margin is not sustainable. We build operations that can run season after season without breaking the people in them.
- **Equity** - We are striving to be more giving, equitable, and inclusive - internally and externally. Our hourly teams are treated with the same respect as our executive team. Bilingual leadership matters. Every voice in the operation deserves a hearing.

## The Pillars - What Holds the Company Up

Four pillars. The strategic frame for every decision the company makes.

- **KitchFix Is a Great Place to Work.** We attract and keep people because the work is good, the team is good, and the company invests in their growth. Culture, training, retention, recognition. Leadership is the lever.
- **We Are Proactive vs Reactive.** We see what is coming. We staff for the homestand before the homestand. We surface issues to the client before the client surfaces them to us. We do not react to fires we should have prevented.
- **We Are Growing.** More accounts, more leagues, more teams. Growth is how we create new opportunities for the people on this team - promotions, new responsibilities, a bigger company to belong to. Sales and renewals are how growth happens.
- **We Are Profitable.** Margin discipline is what makes everything else possible. Sustainable food cost, sustainable labor, accurate AP/AR, scope discipline at the client level. A profitable site is a site that gets reinvested in. An unprofitable site gets cut.

## Daily Practice - How We Show Up

The Hospitality Promise is what we sell. How we show up is how we deliver it. Three commitments shape every interaction - with the client, with each other, with the work itself.

### We extend the same hospitality internally that we sell externally.

The standard we hold for clients is the standard we hold for each other. The cook who joined last week deserves the same care, attention, and respect as the GM of a major league team. Senior people stay teachable. Junior people are heard. We coach in the moment. We recognize the work. We discipline privately. We do not pull rank to win arguments.

The hospitality we are known for starts inside our own kitchens, our own offices, our own conversations - or it does not exist at all.

### We solve before we explain.

When something breaks, the first move is to fix it. Defensiveness, blame, and explanation come second to making the situation right for the person in front of us - whether that person is a client, a teammate, or a player walking through the dining room.

After the fix, we look at the system that allowed the break and do the work to keep it from happening again. Honest acknowledgment plus a real fix is what we are paid for.

### We earn trust by being trustworthy.

Trust is built in the small moments where no one is watching. The walk-in we close properly at midnight. The conversation we keep in confidence. The promise we make on Tuesday and keep on Friday.

We are guests in our clients' facilities and stewards of their information, their players, and their reputations - and we treat that responsibility as a privilege. The standards we hold around confidentiality, conduct, and integrity are the price of being in the building.

## Non-Negotiables - What We Will Not Compromise On

Some things flex. Menus adjust to the season. Schedules shift with the game times. Standards adapt to the facility. The items below do not flex.

### Trust

Confidentiality, integrity, and honesty with the client and with each other. The conversation we keep. The number we report accurately. Trust is the foundation underneath every account we run, and once broken it is rarely rebuilt. AGR-001 The Big Rules holds the working detail.

### The Standard

The Hospitality Promise is the floor, not the ceiling. <Fact id="brand_promise" /> is what every leader is expected to deliver - across accounts, across seasons, across the inevitable hard days. Effort does not substitute for outcome.

### The Team

Every person we work with - hourly, salaried, client-side, KitchFix-side - is treated with the same care we extend to clients. Pulling rank, blaming the line, hiding behind hierarchy: not how we operate. The leadership team is in service of the people doing the work, not the other way around.

### Genuine Hospitality

Hospitality is the company. Not a tagline, not a service add-on, not something we turn on for the client and off for the team. A leader who cannot hold genuine hospitality across both is in the wrong seat.

# Culinary Philosophy

Who we are as a culinary company. The four Values that define KitchFix culinary - Intentional, Authentic, Responsible, and Inspiring - apply in the kitchen, on the menu, on the line, and in the dining room every day.

- **Intentional** - We cook with purpose. Every menu is designed to fuel the team we serve. Every dish is on the line because it earns its place. Every ingredient is chosen for what it brings to the plate - nutrition, flavor, authenticity, satisfaction.
- **Authentic** - Authentic is the bar that separates KitchFix from generic contract food service. If we cook a cuisine, we cook it the way the people who built that cuisine would recognize it - ingredients, technique, presentation, and intent. Authenticity is also why players from every background feel at home at our table.
- **Responsible** - Every plate earns its place. Our chefs only send out dishes they would proudly eat themselves - flavor, presentation, and execution all matter. Nutrition, craveability, food safety, and budget stewardship are our responsibility, not the client's. The standards we hold in the kitchen are what turn a delicious meal into a safe one.
- **Inspiring** - We strive to be innovative in our field - staying ahead of trends and bringing cuisines, flavors, and ingredients to the clubhouse line that clients have not seen before. We surround ourselves with like-minded chefs who challenge and inspire each other to be better every day. We educate - not only our staff, but our peers and the guests we feed - making dining in our operation fun, memorable, and informative.

# Client Promise

What every leader is accountable to make true for every client we serve. The operating commitment behind the Hospitality Promise.

## What Every KitchFix Client Gets

The Hospitality Promise - <Fact id="brand_promise" /> - is what we sell. The four commitments below are how we deliver it. Every account, every season, every shift.

### A partner, not a vendor.

We do not show up to fulfill a contract. We show up to make the team we serve better at what they do. The dietitian gets a partner who executes their standards without negotiation. Athletic Training and S&C get fuel for training, recovery, and travel. Clubhouse staff gets a team that integrates with the facility and the season. Vital partner is not the title on the contract. ### Food the team can trust.

Every dish is chef-crafted and aligned with the dietitian's standards. Allergen and food safe by protocol, not by accident. Hot food hot, cold food cold, presented to a standard the chef would put their name on. We take pride and responsibility in fueling the teams we serve.

The best service is the service the client never has to ask for. We staff for peak before the peak hits. We surface issues before the client does. When something breaks, we fix it before the client notices, and we name it honestly when we cannot. Defensiveness is not a service posture. Solutions are.

### Hospitality, every interaction.

Every player, coach, and staff member in our space is treated as someone who matters. By name when possible. By attention always. The fifteenth meal of the homestand is delivered with the same care as the first. Hospitality is not a moment we engineer. It is the temperature of the room we run.

# Site Leadership

Regional and site leadership that serve our clients every day and live our Hospitality Promise.

## Read First - How This Section Works

This section defines the five leadership roles that run the operation: the Regional Director of Operations, the Site Leader, the Sous Chef, the Hospitality Manager, and the Corporate Field Chef.

Each role is documented in two layers.

- Role Overview - one page, the position at a high level, the leadership essentials.
- Role Definition - the full responsibility set, used for alignment, performance reviews, and development.

## The Five Roles - Who Owns What

The reporting structure top to bottom. Every role has a clear owner, a clear reporting line, and a clear scope.

| Role | Owns | Reports To |
|---|---|---|
| Regional Director of Operations | All sites in the region. Regional P&L. Client relationships. Develops Site Leaders. Holds the region to the SLA. | VP of Operations |

## The Six Themes - How Every Role Is Evaluated

Six areas of leadership, in priority order. People first because it is the leverage. Compliance lasts because it is the regulatory floor every leader holds, not the differentiator that builds great operations.

| Area of Leadership | What It Covers |
|---|---|
| People | Hire, train, coach, develop, manage labor, succession, certifications, recognition. |
| Operations | Run the daily operation. Service on time. Schedule, time clocks, equipment, maintenance, food safety logs, daily sanitation, internal communications. |

## Regional Director of Operations - Role Overview

Supports Site Leaders. Coordinates across site, corporate, and client. Assesses against the standard and drives the fix.

### Fundamentals

- Leader - Lead the region. Hold the standard. Make the call when the call needs making.
- Account Manager - Hold the account-level relationship with the client. Senior voice, not day-to-day.
- Standard Assessor - Walk every site. Assess against the SLA. Surface gaps. Drive corrective action to closure.
- Multi-Stakeholder Coordinator - Move information between site, corporate, and client. Translate between the three. Make sure nothing falls through the cracks.

### Main Objective

Support Site Leaders in their goals. Understand both client and KitchFix standards at a high level. Identify opportunities, communicate internally, and drive solutions. Make critical decisions when necessary.

### Characteristics

Humble - Inquisitive - Solves complex problems - World-class communicator - Decisive - Empathetic

### Six Themes

- People - Support Site Leaders in hiring, training, coaching, and off-boarding. Lead Site Leader performance reviews. Succession planning. HR point of contact for the region.
- Operations - Expert in KitchFix tools. Step in when an issue prevents the Site Leader from functioning. Hand off to the Task Force when extended coverage is required. Daily communication with VPO.
- Financial - Expert on the regional P&L. Lead period financial reviews with each site. Investigate opportunities. Support yearly budget planning.
- Client - Senior account voice. Build relationships with key stakeholders. Solicit feedback on the Site Leader. Surface innovation and sales opportunities to VPO.
- Culinary - Hold SLA-level food quality. Loop in the Director of Culinary on menu review and special events. Vendor support.
- Compliance - Hold sites to company-wide standards. Participate in alignments. Drive adoption of company rollouts.

### Reports To / Assessed By

Reports to VP of Operations. Assessed annually post-EOY (December/January) per SOP-001 Leadership Performance System.

## Regional Director of Operations - Role Definition

Supports Site Leaders. Coordinates across site, corporate, and client. Assesses against the standard and drives the fix.

### Role

The RDO reports to the VPO and supports all sites in the assigned region (East or West). Partners with the Director of Culinary on menu and culinary standards across the region, with HR on hiring, onboarding, and personnel matters, and with the corporate auditor on operational alignments. Manages the duality of being part site-leader, part-corporate, part-client at all times.

### People

**Support Site Leaders**

Support Site Leaders in hiring, training, coaching, and off-boarding hourly staff. Hold ongoing leadership development with each site's Leadership Team.

Be the direct point of contact for HR issues escalated by the Site Leader or surfaced by hourly staff. Lead succession planning for the Site Leader at every site.

**Hire salaried managers**

Participate in the hiring process for every Sous Chef and Hospitality Manager hire across the region. Interview candidates directly. Partner with HR and Site Leader on offers and onboarding.

**Performance management**

Lead the performance review process for each Site Leader on the cadence their contract type requires (PDC bi-annual; MLB Clubhouse and MiLB annual at season close), per SOP-001.

Participate in performance reviews for salaried managers (Sous and HM) led by the Site Leader. Document coaching and corrective conversations in writing.

**Surface red flags**

Identify issues or potential red flags with site leadership and communicate them to the VPO before they become formal performance issues.

Partner with the VPO on any termination or formal performance management.

### Operations

**Tool expertise**

Be an expert in every KitchFix tool the operation uses, including Operations Hub (Invoice Capture, Vendor Manager, Inventory Input, Homestand & Labor Tracker, Team Directory & Dashboard), HR People Portal (PAF, New Hire), AP/AR, Google Suite (Drive, Calendar, Email), Rippling, Greenhouse, and Slack.

Master and train on tools as they are developed. Coach Site Leaders on tool adoption and proper use. Hold the standard for tool data quality across the region.

**Step-in capacity**

Be available to step in and lead site operations when an issue prevents the Site Leader from functioning. Manage the duality of being part site-leader, part-corporate, part-client during step-in periods.

When functioning as a chef on the line, wear appropriate kitchen gear.

**Partner with Corporate Field Chefs**

Be capable of partnering with and handing off site leadership to a Corporate Field Chef when extended coverage is required. Work with the VPO to provide explicit scope, duration, and standards in writing. Brief the Corporate Field Chef on the site's standards, SLA, and current state.

**Internal communications**

Maintain regular and healthy communication with the VPO across all operational updates - text, Slack, calls, email.

Hold a weekly 1:1 with each Site Leader in the region. Hold a weekly 1:1 with the VPO. Participate in monthly regional financial review with the VPO.

**Site presence and presentation**

Be present at every site on a published cadence - monthly minimum during operating season, more during ramp and Spring Training. When on site, present professionally and match the team's standard.

Participate in the site's daily pre-service when on site.

### Financial

**Own the regional P&L**

Be the expert on the regional P&L - the sum of site P&Ls in the region. Know every site's financials line by line. Speak to variance with confidence.

**Period financial reviews**

Hold period financial reviews with each Site Leader in the region. Partner with the Site Leader on period-end variance and corrective action.

Run a monthly regional financial review with the VPO.

**Drive financial alignment**

Investigate opportunities and provide support, guidance, and direction to align sites with financial targets. Flag risk early.

**Support budget planning**

Support yearly budget planning with the Senior Leadership Team. Roll up the regional budget to VPO with commentary.

### Client

**Account management**

Be the regular touch point for the client to assess our account-level performance. Identify, engage, and build relationships with key client-side stakeholders: Clubhouse Manager, Director of Performance Nutrition, Athletic Training, Strength and Conditioning, Front Office.

**Client meetings**

Ensure weekly/bi-weekly meetings happen between the Site Leader and client at every account. Participate in those meetings on a regular cadence.

**Feedback loop**

Solicit client feedback regarding the Site Leader and the support team. Share feedback with the VPO. Build action plans with the Site Leader to address feedback.

**Co-lead account renewals**

Co-lead renewal conversations with the VPO. Partner with the Site Leader on renewal preparation: scope, pricing, and operational story.

**Innovation**

Participate in and encourage innovation ideas led by Site Leaders. Communicate developments back to the VPO. Lead client presentations of innovation when scoped.

### Culinary

**Hold the food standard**

Ensure the food quality and standards outlined in the SLA are upheld at every site in the region. Walk every site for culinary standards on every visit.

**Partner with the Director of Culinary**

Involve the Director of Culinary in the menu review process at every site. Ensure Director of Culinary review and approval before client distribution.

**Vendor support**

Be a support for Site Leaders when vendor issues arise. Site Leader owns the vendor relationship; RDO supports escalations and resolution.

**Special events**

Assist with special events as requested by the client. Involve the Director of Culinary in menu development for special events.

### Compliance

**Hold company-wide standards**

Hold every site in the region to company-wide standards. Same standard at every site, except where the SLA explicitly carves out an account-level override.

**Compliance scope**

Support Site Leaders across the full compliance picture: allergen management (KitchFix Allergen Playbook), state and local regulations, ServSafe currency, MSDS, HACCP, food safety logs, business licensing.

**Audits and Alignments**

Participate in operational audits and alignments with corporate auditors. Partner with sites to meet audit standards and to close findings.

**Drive adoption of company rollouts**

Drive adoption of company rollouts at every site in the region. Hold Site Leaders accountable to adoption deadlines.

**Marketing and sales**

Adhere to branding in all client-facing communications. Receive and flag internal and external sales opportunities to the VPO.

**Adherence to Confidentiality Agreement**

See AGR-001 The Big Rules (standalone document).

## Site Leader - Role Definition

Site Leader (Executive Chef or General Manager). The leader in charge of the operation.

### Role

The Site Leader reports to the Regional Director of Operations (East or West) and manages the site Leadership Team of Sous Chef and Hospitality Manager. Owns the site, the team, the client relationship, the P&L, and the SLA. Leads daily operations across all six themes.

### People

**Hire Staff**

Hire all hourly site staff - Cooks, FOH Attendants, Dishwashers - through Greenhouse: post, source, interview, reference, offer, onboard.

**Hire The Right Support Leaders**

Sous Chef and Hospitality Manager are not interchangeable backfills. The wrong hire shows up in food cost, labor cost, client trust, and the Leadership Team. RDO participates in every salaried hire.

**Form a Cohesive Leadership Team**

Run the weekly Leadership Team meeting (SL + Sous + HM). Present one direction to the team - no BOH/FOH separation.

**Train**

Run structured onboarding for every new hire: paperwork, ServSafe and ServSafe Allergens, new hire orientation, allergen orientation, role-specific training, shadow shifts.

**Coach**

Coach in the moment. Corrections happen at the line, not after. Run weekly 1:1s with the Sous Chef and the Hospitality Manager. Document corrective conversations in writing.

**Manage Labor**

Build the schedule against forecasted volume using service calendars, the labor budget, and Rippling forecast. Publish the schedule one week prior at minimum.

Manage Rippling punches daily - correct missed punches, document edits, resolve exceptions before payroll closes.

**Hold ServSafe Currency**

Hold every employee (salaried and hourly) to ServSafe Food Handlers and ServSafe Allergens. No certification, no work.

**Offboard**

Run a structured offboarding for every separation: PAF paperwork submitted, equipment and keys and access returned and logged, knowledge transfer documented.

**HR Escalation**

First stop for site-staff HR issues: complaints, concerns, conflicts, accommodations. Document HR matters in writing as they arise. Escalate to RDO and HR per AGR-001.

**Succession Planning**

Maintain backfill plans for site leadership should you be out one day, one week, one month. Train Sous and HM on Site Leader responsibilities.

**Culture Commitments inside People**

Attend the yearly leadership retreat. Translate retreat outcomes into specific site actions. Participate in KitchKudos and recognition rituals.

### Operations

**Run Service Every Day**

Run the daily meal rhythm to the client schedule - breakfast, arrival, BP, pregame, postgame. Adjust the daily plan in advance, not in the moment.

**Schedule**

Build and publish the schedule one week prior at minimum. Build to forecast volume, the labor budget, and the meal rhythm. Create coverage for peak and ramp before they hit.

**Time Clocks Management**

Manage Rippling punches daily - correct missed punches, document edits, resolve exceptions before payroll closes. Hold the team to clean punching.

**On-time Standard**

Every meal period hits its service window. Every shift starts ready. The Site Leader does not need to be the one who unlocks the door, but the operation hits the start time on time.

**Maintenance & Repair**

Walk the site daily. Log issues as you find them, and close them proactively. Manage hood cleaning, equipment repair, pest control, deep cleans, and facility issues through Operations Hub.

**Internal Communications**

Run a weekly Leadership Team meeting with the Sous Chef and Hospitality Manager - the working session that aligns the three-person Leadership Team for the week ahead.

Hold a weekly 1:1 with each direct report. Hold a weekly 1:1 with the RDO.

**Pre-shift as a Ritual**

Run pre-service every shift as the cultural reset point of the day, not just a logistics meeting. Hold every team member to the same standard.

**Daily Food Safety Logs**

Maintain HACCP and food safety logs daily - receiving, cooling, holding, temperature, sanitizer, and any client-required documentation.

### Financial

**Budget Proactively**

Schedule to the labor budget. Order to the food budget and the menu. Flag fiscal concerns to the RDO. Build next-season budget recommendations off real operating data.

**Read Your P&L**

Read and understand the site P&L line by line. Know what every line item represents and what drives variance. Speak to the P&L with the team and the client.

**Hit Cost Targets**

Manage food cost to the account's target percentage. Manage labor to target, applying the labor formula where applicable. Variance flagged with commentary.

**Steward the Client's Budget**

Treat every dollar of the client's spend as if it were KitchFix's own. Do not approve spend the client wouldn't approve.

**Report Financials**

Lead the period review with revenue, food cost percentage, labor cost percentage, contribution margin, and variance commentary.

**AP/AR Ownership**

AP/AR is owned by the Site Leader and is accountable for accuracy.

**Run Financial Tools**

Route every vendor invoice through Operations Hub Invoice Capture weekly. Fix rejected invoices in a timely fashion. Approve POs and payment requests through the proper workflow.

# Accountability System

Financial reviews, performance reviews, operational audits, end-of-year close, retreat, 1:1 cadence. Built around the operating cycle of each contract type.

## What This Document Is

The Accountability System is the annual cadence of reviews and audits that hold the operation to standard. Five tracks. Different cadences. One purpose: keep the company aligned with the Hospitality Promise.

> ANCHOR: The audit is corporate alignment, not corporate enforcement.

Its purpose is to give the field team consistent, authentic feedback they can act on, give corporate the insight to communicate confidently with clients, and build the muscle of consistent performance reviews so they become a normal part of how we operate.

## Five Tracks

Five distinct tracks, each with its own cadence, lead, and output. They are not interchangeable. Financial reviews assess the numbers. Performance reviews assess the leader. Operational audits assess the operation. EOY closes the cycle. Retreat resets the company.

| Track | Cadence | Led By | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial Reviews | Period-end + EOY | Site Leader and RDO | P&L |
| Performance Reviews | Contract-type cadence: PDC bi-annual; MLB Clubhouse + MiLB annual. RDO + Corporate Field Chef annual. | Reviewer (one level up); Oversight (one level above) | Cycle Performance Review; 90-Day WOW Plan for new-role transitions |
| Operational Audits | Self-audit + corporate audit, two waves per cycle | Self: RDO + Site Leadership. Corporate: Ops Lead | Audit report; corrective action within one week |
| EOY | Per cycle close: PDC late October, MLB after last homestand or playoffs | RDO + Site Leader; VPO oversight | EOY review, shutdown checklist, performance review |
| Retreat | December (MLB winter meetings) | Senior Leadership | Site Leaders, RDOs, Corporate only |

Most signals from any one track surface in weekly 1:1s. Formal escalation is reserved for patterns of misses across multiple tracks where direct coaching has not produced change.

**Regional period review**

Held at the close of period 13.

**Regional year-end**

RDO and VPO walk the full-year regional roll-up. Inputs to renewal preparation. Inputs to next-year budget.

## Performance Reviews

### The 90-Day WOW Plan - Event-Triggered

Used when a leader enters a new role: new hire, internal promotion, or lateral move into a new account. One-time. Day 1 through Day 90, with a structured first 30 days, week-over-week priorities, and a Day-90 transition into the regular Cycle Performance Review.

Not required for routine role continuation, return from leave, or reassignment within the same account at the same level. Those leaders stay on the regular Cycle cadence.

Recurring once a leader is established in role. Cadence depends on contract type. Self-assessment and reviewer assessment with a rating scale, written commentary, and forward-looking priorities.

## Operational Audits

Two corporate audit waves per year per contract type. Each is preceded by a self-audit so the team can correct before the corporate visit. The sequence is the engine that builds the muscle.

### Self-Audit

Run by the RDO in partnership with the Site Leader. Same rubric the corporate audit will use. Report sent to VPO, Director of Culinary, Senior Director of Operations.

The self-audit does the heavy lifting. By the time corporate arrives, the team has already seen the gaps and started the fix. The corporate audit becomes a calibration, not a discovery.

### Corporate Audit

Run by Corporate Leadership. Same rubric as the self-audit. The report goes to VPO, RDO, and Site Leaders. Within three weeks of the self-audit.

VPO and RDO assess and work with the team immediately following the report - no waiting for the next cycle.

### Audit Output

Findings categorized: Met, Partially Met, Not Met. Corrective actions assigned with owner and due date. Repeat findings escalated to VPO. Findings feed performance reviews and inform development priorities.

This section defines how KitchFix engages clients across the year. Two distinct rhythms run in parallel.

- The Client Management Cadence - always running. Bi-weekly check-ins between RDO, Site Leader, and client. Four quarterly reviews timed to the operating cycle. Surveys ahead of in-person reviews.
- The Renewal Cadence - activated 90 days before each contract expires. The structured commercial conversation that closes the next year of the relationship.

> ANCHOR: Renewals are the commercial endpoint of the Leadership OS. Every role definition, every audit, every scorecard exists in part to make us defensible at renewal.

## Client Management Cadence

The cadence that runs every year, every account. Three layers - bi-weekly, quarterly, periodic - feeding each other so the client always knows where they stand and we always know where they stand.

### Bi-Weekly Check-Ins

The operating heartbeat of the client relationship. Held jointly by the RDO and Site Leader, with the client at the table.

**Cadence**

Minimum bi-weekly. More frequent during high-volume periods (Spring Training ramp, FCL, MLB peak homestands) when the operation is moving fast.

**Attendance, KitchFix side**

RDO and Site Leader together. The RDO holds the senior account voice; the SL holds the day-to-day operating voice. Both are at the table.

**Attendance, client side**

Primary KitchFix point of contact (typically the team's dietitian or operations lead). Other client stakeholders attend as the agenda requires.

**Standing agenda**

The agenda repeats every meeting so nothing falls through the cracks. Last meeting's action items, current operating issues, upcoming homestand or operating window, scope or scheduling changes, anything escalating.

### Quarterly Reviews

Four anchor reviews per year. Each one timed to a transition point in the operating cycle - entering Spring Training, exiting it, the annual ABR, and EOY.

| Review | Timing | Purpose | Inputs / Outputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-ST Review (PDC) / Pre-Season Review (MLB) | On-site, ~2 weeks before ramp | Set Spring Training or season expectations together. Walk the facility, confirm scope, surface anything new. | Client and KitchFix on the same page entering ramp. Prep checklist confirmed. |
| Post-ST Review / Post-Season Review | Within 30 days of ST close (April) or season close | How the cycle went. Client survey first, in-person meeting after. SLA updates and field feedback factored in. | Client survey results. SLA updates from the field. Action items for the operating year. |
| ABR / SLA Review | Yearly, scheduled per account | Annual Business Review and full SLA review. The contract-level conversation about scope, performance, value. | Updated SLA. Documented scope changes. Inputs to renewal. |
| EOY Review | After EOY close (PDC late Oct/early Nov, MLB after last homestand or playoffs) | Year-end story to the client. Wins, gaps, what we are carrying into next year. SLA updates and field feedback factored in. | Client survey results (when run). SLA updates. Year-end documentation. Inputs to renewal preparation. |

### Pre-ST Review (PDC) / Pre-Season Review (MLB)

Held on site, roughly two weeks before ramp begins. This is the alignment meeting that sets the cycle in motion. Walk the facility together. Confirm scope. Surface anything new the client is bringing into the season.

### Post-ST Review / Post-Season Review

Held within 30 days of ST close (typically April) or season close. Two-step process. Client survey first; the survey gives the client space to put structured feedback in writing without the dynamics of an in-person meeting. In-person review follows, with SLA updates and field feedback factored in.

> NOTE: Internal vs Client: Post-ST and Post-Season Reviews are the client-facing conversation. They happen in the same 30-day window as the internal Cycle Performance Reviews, but they are separate forums with separate audiences. Internal reviews surface to the leader; client reviews surface to the client.

### ABR / SLA Review

Annual Business Review. The contract-level conversation about scope, performance, value, and any structural changes to the relationship. Held on a date agreed with the client at signing or first ABR.

### EOY Review

Held after the contract type's EOY close (PDC late October or early November, MLB after the last regular-season homestand or end of playoff run). Year-end story to the client.

**Attendance, KitchFix side**

RDO and Site Leader. VPO joins when the renewal opens within 60 days of EOY Review.

**Attendance, client side**

Account-appropriate roster - typically the primary contact (CHM or operations lead), the team RD, and a senior client-side stakeholder.

### Client Surveys

Surveys are how we get honest written feedback before the room conversation. They protect the relationship by giving the client a low-friction channel to put things in writing.

**When surveys run**

- Post-ST or Post-Season - survey first, in-person meeting after. Standard.
- EOY - survey first, in-person meeting after. Standard.

**What surveys cover**

Food quality, service, hospitality, communication, responsiveness, alignment with team needs, areas for improvement, things to keep, things to change.

**How surveys feed the system**

Survey responses are the input to the Post-ST/Post-Season and EOY reviews - they do not replace the in-person meeting. They prepare it.

### How This Cadence Connects

The Client Management Cadence does not run in isolation. It feeds and is fed by the operational audit cycle, the financial review cycle, and renewal preparation.

- **Operational audits feed the quarterly reviews** - Wave 1 audit findings (post-ramp) feed the Post-ST and Post-Season Reviews. Wave 2 audit findings (mid-season) feed the ABR.
- **Financial reviews feed ABR** - Period and year-end financial reviews surface the operating economics. Trends we see internally inform what we discuss with the client at ABR.
- **Quarterly reviews feed renewal** - Post-ST/Post-Season, ABR, and EOY reviews are the inputs to renewal preparation. By the time renewal opens 90 days out, we have most of the working data already in hand.

## Renewal Cadence

Each renewal conversation runs three phases. Built on top of the year-long Client Management Cadence - by the time renewal opens, we have most of the input we need.

- **Preparation (90 days out)** - RDO and SL build the renewal preparation package. State of relationship, operational story, P&L performance, opening commercial position.
- **Engagement (60 days out)** - VPO and RDO begin the conversation with the client.
- **Decision (30-0 days out)** - Final terms negotiated and agreed. Contract executed during the 30-to-0-day window.

### Renewal Leads

VPO leads every renewal conversation. RDO partners on every renewal in their region. SL prepares the operational package and joins the conversation when invited.

# Cadence Matrix

Master reference. Maps every responsibility under each of the six themes to its evaluation cadence, evidence source, and what "meets standard" looks like.

## Grading Scale

- 5 - Exceeds. Becomes the company benchmark.
- 4 - Above target. Reliable. Ready for additional scope.
- 3 - Meets target. The bar.
- 2 - Below target. Coaching required.
- 1 - Significantly below. Performance management territory.

## Cadence Legend

- D - Daily / Real-time.
- W - Weekly.
- P - Period-End.
- Q - Quarterly. Surfaces in operational audits.
- A - Annual / Spring Training cycle.
- as occurs - Event-driven.
- always - Continuous standard.

## How to Use the Matrix with the Scorecard

This matrix is the reference. The Site Leader Scorecard is the tool. At each cadence layer, the RDO pulls the relevant evidence and grades against the standard. Findings feed coaching in 1:1s and the Cycle Performance Review at season close.

> ANCHOR: Coaching system, not punitive. Most signals roll into the weekly 1:1. Formal performance management is reserved for patterns of misses across multiple cadences where direct coaching has not produced change.
