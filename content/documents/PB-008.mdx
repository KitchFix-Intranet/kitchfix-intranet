# Purpose & Scope

Preparing for emergencies before they happen, and protecting people first when they do.

> ANCHOR: In an emergency, people come first - always before food, property, or service. Every procedure in this manual starts there.

This manual covers how every KitchFix site prepares for and responds to emergencies - fire, medical, severe weather, security threats, and utility failures - and how the company keeps critical operations running through a disruption. It applies to every site and every employee, and it works alongside PB-007 Workplace Safety Manual (prevention) and SOP-002 Safety & Incident Management (reporting).

# Emergency Action Plan

Every site has a written Emergency Action Plan. Know it before you need it.

Each site's plan is specific to its building and is reviewed with every employee at onboarding and at least annually. The plan names:

- Primary and secondary exits, and the outside meeting point.
- The alarm - how it sounds and how to trigger it.
- Roles - who calls 911, who accounts for the team, who shuts down equipment if safe.
- Emergency contacts - local emergency services, building or Club facilities, and KitchFix corporate.
- Locations of extinguishers, first-aid kits, AED, and utility shutoffs.

# Evacuation & Accountability

On the alarm or order to evacuate, leave immediately by the nearest safe exit.

- Do not stop for belongings. Shut off equipment only if it is safe and immediate.
- Help anyone who needs assistance to evacuate.
- Go directly to the meeting point. Stay there.
- The senior employee on shift accounts for every team member and reports anyone missing to responders.

> CRITICAL: Never re-enter the building until a fire official or emergency responder - not a manager - clears it. People die going back in for belongings.

# Fire

- Pull the alarm and call 911. Evacuate per section 03.
- Only attempt to fight a small, contained fire if you are trained and have a clear exit behind you - Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep.
- A grease fire is smothered, never watered - hood suppression, a Class K extinguisher, or a lid. (See PB-007 section 08.)
- Shut off gas at the source if it is safe to reach.
- Once out, do not go back in. Account for the team at the meeting point.

# Medical Emergency

Life-threatening symptoms - call 911 first, then notify.

- Call 911 for trouble breathing, severe bleeding, chest pain, loss of consciousness, anaphylaxis, seizure, or a serious burn or fall.
- Render first aid to your level of training. Use the AED if present and trained.
- Send someone to meet and direct EMS to the patient.
- Do not move a person with a possible head, neck, or back injury unless they are in immediate danger.

> NOTE: Every medical emergency is also an incident - report it under SOP-002 once the person is safe. Allergen reactions follow PB-002.

# Severe Weather by Region

KitchFix operates across very different climates. Each site plans for its own.

## Hurricane & Tropical Storm - Florida

Track watches and warnings. Secure or move outdoor and loose items, protect equipment and records, and follow evacuation orders without delay. Coordinate closure and reopening with corporate and the Client.

## Tornado - Midwest & Texas

On a warning, move to the lowest interior room away from windows. Stay until the all-clear.

## Extreme Heat - Arizona, Texas, Florida

Follow the heat-illness protocols in PB-007 section 10. Watch cooling capacity; a cooling-system failure in extreme heat is an emergency.

## Winter Storms - Midwest & Northeast

Plan for closures, travel risk, and heating or pipe failures. Driver safety follows SOP-010.

# Security & Active Threat

Personal safety first. Do not engage.

- For any weapon, threat of violence, or active assault, call 911.
- Run - if there is a safe path out, leave and bring others with you.
- Hide - if you cannot leave, lock or barricade, silence phones, stay low and quiet.
- Fight - only as an absolute last resort, if your life is in immediate danger.
- Notify People Operations and corporate as soon as it is safe. The incident is reported under SOP-002 section 07.9.

> NOTE: No social media, no outside discussion. Client or MLB notification runs through corporate, not the site.

# Utility Failure

Power, water, or gas loss - protect people first, then food, then service.

- Gas leak: shut off if safe, ventilate, evacuate, and call from outside. Do not flip switches or create sparks.
- Power or refrigeration loss: protect the cold chain and make the keep-or-discard call under SOP-015 Emergency Food Safety.
- Water loss or contamination (boil-water notice): follow SOP-015 - stop affected production until resolved.

> NOTE: Safety decisions live here; the food-safety keep-or-discard decisions live in SOP-015 Emergency Food Safety. Use them together.

# Communication During an Emergency

- Internal first: the Site Leader notifies People Operations and corporate before any outside communication.
- One voice out: Client and MLB notification runs through corporate, not the site, to keep messaging consistent.
- Use the established channels - phone for urgent, Slack and email for the record - per the SOP-002 notification matrix.
- No social media and no public comment about an emergency at a KitchFix site.

# Business Continuity

Keeping critical service running, or restoring it fast, when a site is disrupted.

- Critical functions - identify what must keep running for each account - player and clubhouse meals do not stop because a kitchen does.
- Alternate production - plan backup prep - a sister site, a commissary, or an approved vendor - for a kitchen that goes down.
- Vendor backups - know the backup supplier for critical items before you need them.
- Records & data - menus, recipes, and key records are recoverable - not stored only on a single site machine.
- Recovery - define who decides to reopen, the readiness check before reopening, and how the team is brought back.

# Drills & Review

- Run evacuation drills so the team knows the exits and meeting point by habit.
- Review each site's Emergency Action Plan at least annually and whenever the building or operation changes.
- After any real emergency, run a short after-action review - what worked, what to fix - and update the plan.
- Keep emergency contacts, shutoff locations, and equipment locations current.
