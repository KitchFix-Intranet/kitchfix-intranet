# THE AGREEMENT

## Why This Matters

You handle food for elite athletes. A sick food handler can put a roster down. Reporting your symptoms protects them, your coworkers, and you.

> ANCHOR: Some illnesses spread through food even when you feel well enough to work. The law and our standard require you to report them so we can keep food safe. You will never be punished for reporting honestly.

## Symptoms You Must Report

Before you work, tell your manager if you have any of these:

- Vomiting.
- Diarrhea.
- Jaundice (yellowing of the skin or eyes).
- Sore throat with fever.
- An infected cut, wound, or boil on your hands or exposed arms.

## Illnesses You Must Report

Tell your manager right away if a doctor diagnoses you with any of these:

- Norovirus.
- Hepatitis A.
- Shigella.
- Shiga toxin-producing E. coli (STEC).
- Salmonella Typhi (typhoid fever).
- Nontyphoidal Salmonella.

## Exposure

Also report if you were exposed to, or live with someone diagnosed with, any of the illnesses listed above.

## How and When to Report

- Tell your manager before your shift - not after.
- If you are unsure whether something counts, report it anyway.
- You will not be punished for reporting honestly. Reporting is the right thing to do.

## What Happens Next

Depending on your symptoms or diagnosis, you may be restricted from certain work or excluded from the kitchen until you are cleared, following the FDA Food Code. You return to your normal duties once you are cleared.

> CRITICAL: Never work around food while you have vomiting or diarrhea. Report it and go home. This protects players, guests, and your coworkers.
