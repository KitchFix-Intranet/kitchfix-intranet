# Purpose and Scope

How pay increases work at KitchFix - who is eligible, when raises happen, how they are decided, and the rules that keep them fair and funded. The goal is simple: pay that is competitive, consistent, and earned.

> ANCHOR: We pay people fairly for the work they do and the value they bring. Raises are earned through performance and funded by the business - never automatic, never arbitrary.

This Policy applies to all KitchFix employees - hourly and salaried, seasonal and year-round, across every account and every state in which we operate.

It governs how pay is structured, the types of pay increase, who is eligible, the cycle on which raises happen, who approves them, and the compliance rules that apply.

Actual pay ranges are not published in this Policy. They are maintained separately in REF-006 Hourly Pay Bands and REF-007 Leadership Pay Bands, updated on their own schedule as the market and minimum-wage law change.

> NOTE: This Policy is not a contract and does not alter the at-will employment relationship. It does not guarantee any increase. All increases are subject to performance, business results, and budget.

# How Pay Is Structured

Every role sits in a pay band - a defined range for that role in that market. Bands are the foundation that keeps pay fair across people, accounts, and states.

## Pay Bands by Role

Each role - Dishwasher, Cook, FOH Attendant, Sous Chef, Hospitality Manager, Executive Chef - has a pay band with a minimum, a midpoint, and a maximum. Hourly bands are maintained in REF-006 Hourly Pay Bands; leadership bands in REF-007 Leadership Pay Bands.

## Geography Matters

Bands are set by market. Arizona, Florida, Texas, and New York carry different minimum wages and labor markets, so the band for a role in one state will not match another. Every rate must respect the minimum wage that applies where the employee works.

## Position in Band

Where an employee sits in their band reflects experience and sustained performance:

- Lower third - new to the role, or still building proficiency.
- Midpoint - fully proficient and meeting the standard consistently.
- Upper third - sustained excellence, or hard-to-replace tenure and skill.

Band position gives every leader the same yardstick. It is how we prevent compression - the problem of a long-tenured returner earning close to a first-day hire.

## Rates Outside Band

Occasionally an employee's rate falls outside the band for their role and market. Two cases, handled differently:

- Above band (red-circled) - an employee whose rate sits above the band maximum - for example, carried over from a prior arrangement - is red-circled: the rate is protected, not reduced. A red-circled employee is not eligible for further merit increases until the band rises to include their rate. They remain eligible for market or structural adjustments when the whole band moves.
- Below band - an employee whose rate falls below the band minimum is brought up to at least the band floor through an equity adjustment under Section 03.

Grandfathered rates are a closed group. All new hires and season returners are brought in within band.

# Types of Pay Increase

Not every raise is the same. Naming the type keeps each decision clear and fundable.

- Merit - a performance-based increase, earned through a documented review. This is the primary raise type at KitchFix.
- Market / Structural - an adjustment to keep bands current as minimum wage and the labor market move. Not performance-based - it keeps us competitive and compliant.
- Promotion - an increase that comes with moving into a higher role and its band - for example, Cook to Sous Chef.
- Equity - a People-Operations-led correction of an internal disparity or compression. Initiated by People Operations, not requested by the employee.
- Retention - a rare, leadership-approved increase to retain a critical employee. Tightly controlled and not a standard path.

# The Raise Cycle

When raises happen depends on whether the role follows the season or the calendar.

> ANCHOR: For seasonal employees, the raise is the return offer. We evaluate at season's end, decide who we invite back, and the rate we bring them back at is the increase. Rates then hold for the season.

## Seasonal Hourly and Leadership

Most KitchFix roles begin and end with the baseball season. For these employees, pay increases are determined at the end of the season and applied to the next season's return offer. Rates are set for the season and do not change mid-season.

## Year-Round Employees

Corporate and year-round employees are reviewed on an annual cycle tied to the company budget year, so that any increase is planned and funded alongside budget.

## Mid-Season Changes

Mid-season rate changes are the exception, not the rule. A mid-season increase is made only for a genuine promotion - for example, filling a leadership vacancy - and requires VP Ops, RDO, and People Operations approval. Mid-season changes disrupt account budgets and client billing, so they are kept rare and deliberate.

# Eligibility and Criteria

Merit increases are earned. The criteria below tie a raise to standards we already hold.

## Eligibility

- Completed at least one full season - or one full review period for year-round roles - in good standing.
- Not on an active disciplinary track. A Level 3 action, final written warning, or PIP under SOP-004 pauses any increase until it is resolved.
- Required certifications current - ServSafe Food Handler and ServSafe Allergen.
- A performance review on file for the period.

## What Earns It

Merit is based on documented performance, not tenure alone. The substance is the work: the brand promise - <Fact id="brand_promise" /> - delivered consistently; reliability and attendance under POL-004; food safety; and, for leaders, the results on their scorecard.

> NOTE: Performance reviews are the engine of merit. Hourly reviews follow SOP-007; leadership reviews follow SOP-001. A merit increase is not made without a review on file.

# Decision Authority and Approvals

Every increase moves through a defined chain: recommendation, approval, validation, then the system. Recommendations are filed via FORM-007 in the People Portal (People Portal > PAF > Pay Increase Recommendation).

## Authority Matrix

| Increase | Recommends | Approves | Validates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hourly merit / market | Executive Chef | RDO / VP Ops | People Ops + Accounting |
| Leadership merit (Sous, HM, EC) | RDO | Sr Dir Ops / VP Ops | People Ops + Accounting |
| Promotion | EC or RDO | Sr Dir Ops / VP Ops | People Ops + Accounting |
| Equity / market correction | People Operations | Sr Dir Ops / VP Ops + Finance | People Ops |
| Retention | RDO | VP Ops + Sr Dir Ops | People Ops + Finance |

## The Funding Gate

No increase is final until it is funded. Every account has a compensation budget set during planning, and what the client contract supports - including CPI escalators and renewal terms - is the ceiling. The VP of Operations and Senior Director of Operations validate that a proposed increase fits the account's budget before it is approved. We do not fund raises the account economics cannot carry unless leadership chooses that trade-off with full visibility.

Once approved and validated, the increase is entered in Rippling with its effective date.

# Guardrails and Compliance

The rules that keep pay decisions fair, legal, and defensible.

- Stay within band - every increase keeps the employee within the role's pay band. Any exception requires VP Ops and People Operations sign-off.
- Respect wage floors - every rate meets the minimum wage that applies where the employee works. A minimum-wage increase may require a market adjustment to the band - a structural change, not a merit raise.
- Pay transparency - where the law requires it - including New York - pay ranges are disclosed in job postings. People Operations maintains compliant language.
- Document everything - every increase is recorded in Rippling with its type, amount, percentage, effective date, rationale, and approver. That record is our pay-equity defense.
- No promises - raises are merit- and budget-based. No leader promises or implies a guaranteed increase, consistent with the at-will relationship described in PB-004.

# Communication

Open about the process. Private about the numbers.

Every employee should understand how raises work at KitchFix - the cycle, the types, and the criteria. Knowing the process builds trust and answers the question before it is asked.

Individual pay and increase amounts are confidential, and leaders do not discuss one employee's pay with another. Nothing in this Policy restricts an employee's right to discuss their own pay or working conditions.

> ANCHOR: People do not need to know what others earn. They need to know that the way pay is decided is fair, consistent, and the same for everyone. That is the commitment.
