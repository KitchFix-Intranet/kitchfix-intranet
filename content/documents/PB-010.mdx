# Purpose & How to Use

The operating system every KitchFix site runs on - so a site is great because of how it works, not just who's working.

> ANCHOR: A site is great when the rhythm is the same every day, regardless of who's on. This manual is that rhythm. Learn it, run it, and the standard holds whether the EC is on the line or on a plane.

This manual defines how a KitchFix site operates day to day - the daily cadence, who owns what, and the standards every shift is held to. It applies to every site and every contract type. It sets the floor; site-specific protocols - the Club's rules, your kitchen's layout, the account's menu - sit on top of it, and your Executive Chef trains you on those. This Manual sets the floor; each site is responsible for the specifics beyond this expectation.

- This manual runs the site - the rhythm, roles, and operating standards of a shift.
- Food safety lives in SOP-008 - temperatures, handling, and sanitation are governed there and summarized here, not duplicated.
- Safety lives in PB-007; incidents in SOP-002 - how to work safely, and what to do when something goes wrong.

# Site Leadership & Roles

Every site runs through a leadership team, with one clear person in charge every shift.

The chain is simple: the Regional Director of Operations (RDO) oversees the region, the Executive Chef or General Manager runs it, the Sous Chef and Hospitality Manager lead each area, and the hourly team executes.

## Executive Chef / General Manager (EC/GM)

Owns the site - the food, the team, safety, the client relationship, and hitting the operating and financial targets. The EC/GM sets the standard and is accountable for it.

## Sous Chef

Owns kitchen production and the line. Supports the EC/GM and runs the site when the EC/GM is off.

## Hospitality Manager

Owns the front of house - service, presentation, and the guest and player experience.

## Hourly team

Cooks, FOH attendants, and dishwashers - the people who execute the standard every shift, in their station, to spec.

> NOTE: Every shift has one clear person in charge. If the EC is out, the Sous leads. Nobody should ever wonder who's running the shift.

# The Daily Rhythm

The same cadence every day: open, prep, service, reset.

A site runs on a predictable daily arc. The details flex by account, but the rhythm holds everywhere.

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| Open | Unlock and set up, log temperatures, ready every station, check the team in. |
| Prep | Mise en place and production to the day's menu and counts. |
| Huddle | Pre-service huddle - menu, notes, VIPs, assignments, safety. |
| Service | Execute the menu to spec; deliver the hospitality; keep the line and the room clean. |
| Reset / Close | Break down, store and label, clean down, secure, and log the day. |

## The pre-service huddle

Every service starts with a short huddle led by the EC or person in charge: today's menu and specials, any player or client notes, station assignments, and one safety or food-safety reminder. Two minutes that prevent an hour of problems.

# Opening the Site

A consistent open sets up the whole day. Run the checklist, every day, in order.

1. Security & access - unlock, disarm, and confirm the site is secure and as it was left.
2. Power up - lights, hood, equipment, and refrigeration; confirm everything is running.
3. Temperatures - log walk-in, cooler, and freezer temps before anything else (SOP-008, TPL-018).
4. Sanitation setup - sanitizer buckets, handwash stations stocked, surfaces wiped.
5. Station setup - each station built and stocked for the day's menu.
6. Review - menu, counts, and any client or player notes for the day.
7. Team - check the staff in (Rippling), confirm coverage, run the huddle.

> CRITICAL: Temperatures are logged first, every open. A walk-in or cooler out of safe range is a stop-and-escalate - do not load or serve from failed refrigeration. Call the EC and the RDO.

# Service Execution & Hospitality

Service is where the promise is kept: <Fact id="brand_promise" /> - every plate, every interaction.

## The food

- Every item to spec, to temperature, and to presentation standard - no exceptions for pace.
- Hold hot at 135F or above, cold at 41F or below; check and log (SOP-008).
- Replenish before it runs out; a thin or empty line is a missed standard.

## The hospitality

- Read the room. Anticipate needs before you're asked. Be present without being in the way.
- Know the allergens on your line and handle accommodations correctly (PB-002).
- Keep the line and the room clean as you go - hospitality includes what the guest sees.

## The line

Call the kitchen out loud - BEHIND, CORNER, HOT, KNIFE. Communication keeps service safe and fast.

# Closing the Site

A clean, complete close is what makes tomorrow's open easy. Never short the close.

1. Break down service - pull the line, consolidate, and account for product.
2. Store, label, and date - every item cooled, covered, labeled, and dated (SOP-008).
3. Clean down - surfaces, equipment, floors, and the dish area to standard (TPL-019).
4. Refrigeration - walk-in and coolers organized; final temps logged.
5. Equipment - powered down or set as required; nothing left running unsafely.
6. Waste - trash and recycling out; area left clean.
7. Secure - lock up, arm, and confirm the site is closed properly.
8. Log & hand off - complete end-of-day logs; flag anything the EC or next shift needs to know.

# Menu & Production Planning

Plan the food before the shift - what we make, how much, and to whose standard.

## The menu

Each account runs an approved menu. Menus and changes go through the team's Registered Dietitian and the Director of Culinary - some accounts are client-driven (their dietitian sets the standard), some are KitchFix-driven (we lead), per the account's SLA. The menu is never changed unilaterally on site.

## Production planning

Build a daily production list from forecasted covers and the menu. Standardized recipes and production lists keep the food consistent and costed, whoever is on the line. The production list is the bridge between the menu and the order - plan covers, plan production, then order to it (Section 08).

## Recipe management

Recipe management at KitchFix is currently hybrid. Galley is in partial use as a recipe database; broader rollout is paused while the team evaluates longer-term solutions. In practice, chefs also work from their own recipes and from historical recipes maintained across the shared Drive. There is no single recipe system of record today; the Culinary Operating System (PB-006) and the planned culinary platform are intended to consolidate this.

> NOTE: Culinary standards and recipes are owned in the culinary playbook (PB-006 / PB-011). This section is the site's execution of planning - forecasting, producing, and ordering to the menu.

# Inventory, Ordering & Receiving

Have what you need, manage what you have, and know your numbers.

## Pars

Set par levels per account from the menu and production plan, and order to them - not to habit.

## Ordering

Order on the account's cadence to approved vendors, to par and forecast.

## Receiving

- Check every delivery: temperatures on cold and frozen, quality, counts, and the invoice.
- Reject anything out of temp, damaged, or below standard - document it.
- Put away immediately, rotating FIFO (first in, first out).

## Waste

Track waste honestly. It's the fastest read on over-ordering, over-production, and rotation problems.

# Equipment & Smallwares Care

Equipment is how the food gets made - downtime mid-service is a service failure, so care for it before it fails.

- Daily checks - confirm refrigeration, cooking, and warewashing equipment is running right at open; log temps (Section 04).
- Preventive care - clean and maintain equipment per the manufacturer and the cleaning schedule (TPL-019). Small problems caught early stay small.
- Smallwares - knives, pans, and tools are accounted for, maintained, and stored properly.
- Out of service - report any malfunction immediately, tag the equipment out of service, and lock it out where required (PB-007). Never run unsafe equipment.
- Know your contacts - keep equipment manuals and service-contact information accessible before you need them.

> NOTE: A piece of equipment down mid-service is an emergency. Know the workaround and the service contact in advance - not while the line backs up.

# Food Safety & Sanitation

Non-negotiable, every shift. This section points; SOP-008 governs.

> ANCHOR: Food safety is the one area where there is no judgment call and no shortcut. The standards live in SOP-008; running them every shift is the job.

- Temperatures - cook, hold, cool, and store to SOP-008; log on TPL-018. Hot <Fact id="hot_hold_temp" />, cold <Fact id="cold_hold_temp" />.
- Handwashing & hygiene - per SOP-008 and the health-reporting standard - sick staff do not handle food.
- Cross-contamination & allergens - separation, sanitation, and correct allergen handling (PB-002).
- Cleaning - follow the master cleaning schedule (TPL-019); clean as you go.
- Inspection readiness - the site is always inspection-ready (CHK-003), not scrambling when one is announced.

# Staffing, Scheduling & Labor

The right people, scheduled to the business, with accurate time - all in Rippling.

- Scheduling - build the schedule in Rippling to the forecast and the account's needs - enough coverage to hit the standard, no more than the labor target allows.
- Time records - every hour worked is recorded accurately. No off-the-clock work, ever (POL-008).
- Breaks - provided and recorded per policy and the site's state (POL-008).
- Call-outs & coverage - a clear call-out path and a coverage plan so a gap never becomes a service failure.
- Time off - requested and approved in Rippling.

# Team: Hiring, Training & Development

The EC builds the team - hiring to standard, training to competence, and developing people season over season.

## Hiring

Hire to the standard and to the seasonal cadence - the workforce scales with the season (POL-013). The EC and RDO own building the right team for the account.

## Onboarding & training

- Every new hire is onboarded and trained before they work a station solo.
- Food safety, knife-skills verification (FORM-009), and the site's way of working are part of every onboarding.
- Training is documented - if it isn't recorded, it didn't happen.

## Development

Give ongoing feedback, cross-train the team, and grow your people. Rehiring proven staff season to season is how a site keeps its standard without starting over every spring.

> NOTE: The company training and certification program is PB-013; certifications live in Rippling. A site is only as good as its weakest-trained person on the line - train before you deploy.

# Site Financial Discipline

The EC owns the site's P&L - food cost and labor cost are made or lost on the floor every day.

## Know your model

Fee accounts: the client owns food cost and KitchFix manages it. Full-service accounts: KitchFix buys the food under an all-inclusive fee. The model changes how you manage food cost - know which one your account is.

## Food cost

- Order to par and forecast, portion to spec, and control waste (Section 08).
- Store and rotate correctly so product isn't lost to spoilage.

## Labor cost

- Schedule to the labor target and the business (Section 11).
- No off-the-clock work and no unplanned overtime (POL-008).

## Invoices & variances

- Verify every invoice against what was actually received; flag discrepancies.
- Surface budget variances to the RDO early - a small one caught now beats a big one explained later.

> NOTE: Financial operations depth - budgets, reporting, and reconciliation - is owned in PB-009. This section is the daily site discipline that protects the margin.

# Operating in the Client's House

We work in our clients' clubhouses. We are guests - with a job to do at the highest standard.

- Respect the space, the players, and the Club's staff. How we carry ourselves is the brand (POL-014).
- Player health, dietary, and personal information is strictly confidential - never shared or discussed (POL-014).
- The dietitian relationship - some accounts are client-driven (their dietitian sets the standard) and some are KitchFix-driven (we lead). Know which yours is and operate accordingly.
- Communication - keep the client informed through the right channel and the EC - no surprises, clean hand-offs.
- Appearance and conduct meet standard on site, every shift (POL-002).

# Reporting & Communication Cadence

A site that communicates on a rhythm never surprises anyone - not the client, not the RDO.

| Cadence | What's reported |
|---|---|
| Daily | Complete the daily site log (TPL-007); flag any issue the same day. |
| Weekly | Weekly site report to the RDO (TPL-008); the RDO rolls sites up (TPL-009). |
| Client | Regular client check-ins, logged (TPL-011) - the EC owns the client conversation. |
| As needed | Escalate per Section 17 - no problem sits silent. |

> NOTE: The templates already exist. The discipline is using them on cadence - not reconstructing the week from memory when the RDO asks.

# Standards & Self-Audit

The standard is only real if someone checks it - starting with the site itself.

Every site holds the same bar: <Fact id="brand_promise" />. The EC owns that bar and checks the site against it, not once a quarter when a regional visits, but as a habit.

- Daily walk - the EC or person in charge walks the site against standard - line, FOH, storage, sanitation - and corrects on the spot.
- Inspection readiness - run CHK-003 so the site is always ready for a health inspection or a client walk-through.
- Close the loop - a standard that slips and isn't corrected becomes the new standard. Catch it, fix it, and note the pattern.

> NOTE: The client notices a slip before we do unless we're checking ourselves. Self-audit is how we stay ahead of the visit, not behind it.

# Escalation & Support

Know what you can solve, and know exactly when and how to call for help.

Most of a shift is handled on site. Some things are not - and escalating fast is a strength, not a failure.

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Food-safety stop (failed temp, contamination) | Stop, secure the product, and call the EC and RDO immediately (SOP-008). |
| Injury or incident | Follow SOP-002 - care first, then report and notify. |
| Equipment failure | Take it out of service, work around it, and notify the EC and RDO. |
| Staffing crisis (no coverage) | Run the coverage plan; escalate to the RDO if the standard is at risk. |
| Client or player issue | Loop in the EC; the EC owns the client conversation. |

> ANCHOR: When in doubt, escalate. The RDO would rather hear about a problem early than discover it after the client did.
