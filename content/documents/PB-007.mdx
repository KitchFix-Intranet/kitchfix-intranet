# Purpose & Scope

How KitchFix keeps every employee safe at work - the program, the standards, and the responsibilities behind them.

> ANCHOR: A foodservice kitchen is one of the highest-injury workplaces there is - knives, heat, grease, water, and pace, all at once. Safety is a managed program, not a poster on the wall. This manual is how we run it.

This manual defines KitchFix's occupational-safety program, built on OSHA standards and the realities of a working kitchen. It applies to every employee - Executive Chef to dishwasher - at every site, across all contract types and corporate operations. Where a Club or local authority sets a stricter standard, the stricter standard governs.

This manual sets the floor. Site-specific protocols - layout, equipment, Club rules - sit on top of it, and your Executive Chef trains you on those. The standards here apply everywhere, always.

# How to Use This Manual

Prevention lives here. Response lives in SOP-002. Keep the two straight.

This manual is for every employee. The Executive Chef owns safety on site and trains the team to it. It works alongside the rest of the safety system, not on top of it:

- This manual is the program - what safe work looks like at KitchFix - the hazards, the rules, and who owns them.
- SOP-002 handles incidents - when something goes wrong, reporting, severity, notification, and investigation live in SOP-002 Safety & Incident Management. This manual prevents; SOP-002 responds.
- Satellites carry the detail - fleet safety (SOP-010), emergencies (PB-008), and permits and licenses (POL-019) each go deeper than this manual does. Certifications are tracked in Rippling.

> NOTE: If you are looking for how to report an injury, that is SOP-002. If you are looking for how to work without one, you are in the right place.

# Safety Management & Responsibility

Safety is actively managed every shift, owned on site by the leadership triad.

> ANCHOR: Active safety management means catching the hazard before it catches someone - the wet floor, the dull knife, the blocked exit. Every shift: look, correct, document.

## Executive Chef

Owns safety on site. Trains the team, corrects hazards in the moment, and makes sure PPE, certifications, and records stay current.

## Sous Chef & Hospitality Manager

Enforce safe practices in their areas and model them. A leader who walks past a hazard has set the standard at that hazard.

## Every Employee

Works safely, uses required PPE, and reports hazards and near-misses. Reporting in good faith is never punished (see SOP-002). When unsure, stop and ask.

# Hazard Communication & Chemical Safety

Every chemical in the kitchen carries a hazard. Hazard Communication keeps people safe around them.

- Safety Data Sheets - an SDS for every chemical on site, current and accessible to every employee.
- Labeling - every container is labeled. Never store a chemical in an unlabeled container or a food container.
- Storage - chemicals stored away from food and food-contact surfaces; incompatible chemicals kept apart.
- Protection - gloves and eye protection when handling strong chemicals. Follow the label - dilution, contact, ventilation.
- Training - employees are trained on the chemicals they use before they use them.

> NOTE: Food-safety chemical storage - sanitizer concentration, keeping chemicals off the line - is covered in SOP-008 section 10. This section is the worker-safety side: HazCom and SDS access.

# Personal Protective Equipment

PPE is required, not optional, where it is listed. KitchFix provides it and replaces it when worn.

| Task | Required PPE |
|---|---|
| Knife and cutting work | Cut-resistant glove until knife technique is verified (FORM-009); recommended after. Always required on the mandoline, slicer, deboning, shellfish, and blade cleaning. |
| Handling hot pans, racks, or food | Heat-resistant gloves or dry towels - never wet |
| Cleaning and chemical handling | Nitrile gloves and eye protection |
| Dish and warewashing | Waterproof apron and gloves |
| All kitchen and back-of-house work | Slip-resistant shoes |
| Cleaning overhead, hoods, or fixtures | Eye protection |

Use the PPE the task requires every time. If yours is worn out or missing, tell your Executive Chef before you start the task.

# Knife & Equipment Safety

Knives and powered machines cause the most serious injuries in any kitchen. Treat both with full attention.

## Knives

- A sharp knife is safer than a dull one - a dull blade slips. Keep yours sharp.
- Cut on a stable board, away from your body, in full control of the blade.
- Carry a knife tip-down, blade back, and call "KNIFE" as you move.
- Never try to catch a falling knife - step back and let it land.
- Never leave a knife in a sink or under water. Clean it separately, edge away.

## Cut-Resistant Gloves

Cut gloves are competency-based at KitchFix. Until an employee's knife technique is verified on FORM-009 Knife Skills Verification, they wear a cut-resistant glove on the guiding hand for all knife work. Once verified, the glove is recommended for routine knife work, not required.

> CRITICAL: Regardless of verification, a cut-resistant glove is mandatory for everyone, every time, on the mandoline, the slicer, deboning, shucking oysters or shellfish, and cleaning any slicer or food-processor blade.

## Powered Equipment

- Never operate a slicer, mixer, food processor, or dish machine without training on that machine.
- Keep guards in place. Do not defeat or remove them.
- Power off and unplug before cleaning, clearing a jam, or reaching inside.
- No loose clothing, sleeves, or jewelry near moving parts. Report malfunctions and tag the machine out of service.

> CRITICAL: Never reach into or clear a powered machine while it can still turn on. Power off, unplug, and confirm it is dead first. This is how people lose fingers.

Call the kitchen out loud - BEHIND, CORNER, HOT, KNIFE, HEAVY. Be heard to be safe.

# Slips, Trips & Falls

- Clean spills the moment they happen - grease, water, food, ice.
- Put wet-floor signs out during and after mopping, and leave them until the floor is dry.
- Wear slip-resistant shoes - every shift, no exceptions.
- Keep walkways, work areas, and exits clear. No boxes, cords, or pallets across a path.
- Report torn mats, loose tiles, and damaged flooring.
- Use a ladder or step stool to reach height - never a chair, box, or shelf.

# Burns, Heat & Fire Safety

## Burns & Hot Surfaces

- Assume pans, handles, oil, and surfaces are hot. Use dry towels or heat gloves - wet towels transfer heat and burn.
- Warn the line: "HOT BEHIND." Let oil cool before moving or filtering it. Lower food into the fryer gently to avoid splash.

## Fire

- Know where the extinguishers are and how to use them - Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep.
- Know the hood suppression system over the cooking line and how it trips.
- Keep the hood and filters clean (TPL-019). Keep extinguishers and exits clear.
- Smell gas? Shut off the source if safe, ventilate, evacuate, and call from outside.

> CRITICAL: A grease fire is smothered, never watered. Water on burning grease explodes it. Use the hood suppression system, a Class K extinguisher, or a lid - then evacuate if it is not immediately controlled.

# Lifting & Ergonomics

- Lift with your legs, back straight, load held close to your body.
- Test the weight before you commit. If it is heavy or awkward, get a team lift or a cart or dolly.
- Do not twist while carrying - turn your feet, not your spine.
- Store heavy items at waist height. Keep the floor and overhead for light loads.
- Break up repetitive tasks - chopping, scrubbing, scooping - to rest the same muscles.

# Heat Illness & Environmental Safety

Kitchens run hot, and clubhouse and outdoor work adds to it. Heat illness is preventable.

- Hydrate before you are thirsty. Water within reach, all shift.
- Take cool-down breaks. Ease new and returning staff into the heat over their first days.
- Know the signs - cramps, heavy sweating, dizziness, nausea. Confusion, fainting, or hot dry skin is an emergency: move them to cool, call 911.

## Regional Protocols

Geographic conditions are planned per site: hurricane preparedness (Florida), extreme heat (Arizona, Texas, Florida), winter weather (Midwest, Northeast), and wildfire smoke (West). Your site plan and PB-008 carry the specifics.

# Emergency Action & Evacuation

Every site has an Emergency Action Plan. Know it before you need it.

- Know your exits, the alarm, and the outside meeting point.
- In an evacuation, leave immediately. Do not stop for belongings. Help anyone who needs it.
- Go to the meeting point and account for your team.
- Never re-enter the building until a responder or manager clears it.

> NOTE: Full emergency procedures - fire, severe weather, medical, security threat, and utility failure - are in PB-008 Emergency Prep & Continuity.

# Injury & Incident Reporting

When something happens, report it - every injury, every near-miss, every hazard.

- Report everything - injuries, near-misses, and hazards, in good faith. Reporting is never punished (SOP-002).
- Follow SOP-002 - reporting, severity tiers, notification timing, and investigation all live in SOP-002 Safety & Incident Management. This manual does not repeat them.
- First aid on site - a stocked first-aid kit at every site, and an AED where present. Know who on your team is trained.

> CRITICAL: Life-threatening - trouble breathing, severe bleeding, loss of consciousness, anaphylaxis - call 911 first, then notify. Documentation comes after the person is safe.

# Training & Safety Certifications

- New hires are trained on safety before any unsupervised work.
- Equipment training happens before an employee uses that equipment.
- ServSafe and any required certifications are kept current (tracked in Rippling).
- Safety is refreshed regularly, not taught once and filed.
- Training is documented - who, what, when.

> NOTE: Certification tracking lives in Rippling. Food-safety certifications also appear in SOP-008 section 03.

# Recordkeeping & Compliance

- Maintain required safety records - training, SDS, inspections, and incident records (via SOP-002).
- Report any work-related fatality, in-patient hospitalization, amputation, or loss of an eye to OSHA within the required timeframe - the reporting process is in SOP-002.
- Keep site permits and licenses current and tracked per POL-019.
- Keep records accessible on site for audit and inspection.

> NOTE: Severe-incident reporting to OSHA is in SOP-002. Permits and licenses are governed by POL-019. Certifications are tracked in Rippling.

# Workers' Compensation

Work-related injuries are covered. Human Resources coordinates every claim.

- Report work injuries immediately through SOP-002. HR opens the claim.
- The carrier is <Fact id="wc_carrier" />; HR is the claim coordinator.
- No employee is retaliated against for filing a claim.
- Return-to-work is handled per state law and individual circumstance.

> NOTE: Full workers'-comp details - carrier, policy, and state-by-state filing - are in SOP-002 section 10 and the REF-001 Workers' Comp State Annex.
