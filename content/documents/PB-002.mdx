# 01 Purpose and Scope

How KitchFix Performance Food Service identifies, manages, and responds to allergens. Zero acceptable risk. If you are unsure, pause and verify.

This Playbook is the system-of-record for how KitchFix manages allergens across every Performance Food Service account. It defines the Top 9 allergens, the daily non-negotiables that prevent a reaction, the responsibilities of the Site Leader, and the response sequence when a reaction occurs. It supplements but does not replace ServSafe Allergen training, which every team member must hold.

> ANCHOR: There is no acceptable level of risk when it comes to allergens. We never guess. Read labels, verify ingredients, ask the Chef. The standard you walk past is the standard you accept.

## In Scope

- Every team member - BOH and FOH, full-time and seasonal.
- Every meal - breakfast, lunch, dinner, snacks, fueling stations, grab-and-go.
- Every site - PDC, MLB Clubhouse, MiLB. The standard does not change by contract type.
- Every shift - on-the-clock and any time you are inside a Club facility.

## Out of Scope

- Player medical care - governed by Club medical staff. Our role in a reaction is response and notification, not treatment.
- Allergen-free meals or stations - we do not offer them. See Section 04.
- Incident documentation after the fact - governed by SOP-002 Safety and Incident Management.

# 02 The Top 9 Allergens

Every team member must know these by name and know where they hide. A Top 9 posting must be visible in every kitchen.

The FDA recognizes nine major food allergens. The PFS standard adds Sesame to that list - the federal recognition of Sesame as the ninth major allergen took effect in 2023, and it now appears on every KitchFix allergen reference.

> ANCHOR: The most dangerous allergens are the ones that hide. Read labels. Verify ingredients. Do not assume.

## Where Each Allergen Hides

| Allergen | Where It Hides |
|---|---|
| Milk | Butter, cream, cheese, ghee, whey, casein, lactose, ranch, baked goods, chocolate, cream sauces. |
| Eggs | Mayo, aioli, Caesar dressing, fresh pasta, breading, baked goods, egg wash on buns, custard. |
| Fish | Worcestershire sauce, Caesar dressing (anchovy), fish sauce, some Asian broths, surimi. |
| Shellfish | Oyster sauce, some pad Thai, shrimp paste, some stock bases, bouillabaisse. |
| Tree Nuts | Pesto (pine nuts), marzipan, nut oils, nut milks, granola, crusted proteins, some curries. |
| Peanuts | PB, satay sauce, some Asian dressings, energy bars, African stews, cold-pressed peanut oil. |
| Wheat | Bread, pasta, flour tortillas, soy sauce, teriyaki, breading, roux, croutons, couscous. |
| Soy | Soy sauce, teriyaki, tofu, edamame, miso, many dressings, vegetable oil (soybean), lecithin. |
| Sesame | Tahini, hummus, sesame oil, everything seasoning, some breads, halva, falafel. |

## The Anaphylaxis Four

Four of the Top 9 carry the highest risk of anaphylaxis: Fish, Shellfish, Tree Nuts, and Peanuts. A reaction to any of these can become life-threatening in minutes. The other five (Milk, Eggs, Wheat, Soy, Sesame) can also produce severe reactions - do not under-treat them - but the four above are the ones where the response window is shortest.

> NOTE: High risk does not mean only these four matter. It means these four require an even tighter posture on cross-contact, labeling, and storage. A Milk-allergic player can still go into anaphylaxis.

# 03 Daily Non-Negotiables

Five operating disciplines that prevent a reaction. Run them every service. Every site. No exceptions.

## 3.1 Labeling

Every item on the buffet is labeled with "Contains:" followed by the allergens present. A player approaching the line can identify allergens at a glance, without asking.

Acceptable label formats:

- 8.5 by 11 printed signage placed in front of the dish.
- PID (Product Identification) cards placed in front of each dish.
- Written directly on the buffet glass with appropriate marker.

Verification cadence: Chef or Sous Chef walks the line and verifies labels against the master menu before every service. The walk-through is logged in the daily site log.

> ANCHOR: Standard menu format and example signage formats live in PB-006 Culinary OS Handbook. Site Leaders use the standard; do not invent local variants.

## 3.2 Cross-Contact Prevention

Cross-contact is when an allergen-free dish is contaminated by an allergen from another dish or surface. It is the single most common cause of a reaction in kitchens that label correctly.

**Utensil rules**

- Utensils stay with their pan. They do not travel between dishes.
- Tasting spoons are single-use. One taste, one spoon, into the wash.
- Hands are washed between handling allergens and handling allergen-free items - not just gloves changed.

**When cross-contact happens**

- Remove the affected item from the line.
- Refire the dish from scratch with clean equipment.
- Replace utensils and clean the contact surface.
- Notify the Chef immediately.

> CRITICAL: Never remove an allergen from a finished dish - even a garnish, even a single nut, even one crouton. The dish is contaminated. Refire from scratch.

## 3.3 Vendor Substitutions

A vendor swap is one of the most common causes of allergen mislabeling. A product that arrived last week as soy-free may be substituted this week with a soy-containing equivalent and shipped under the same SKU. Catching the swap is the kitchen's responsibility, not the vendor's.

**When a vendor subs a product**

- Do not use the substitute until the allergen status is confirmed against what it replaced.
- If the allergen profile changed, update the menu and all related labels before service.
- Notify the Chef immediately.

> ANCHOR: Never assume a substitute is the same. The label on the case is the source of truth, not the SKU number or the previous order.

## 3.4 Allergen Storage

- Allergen-containing ingredients stored below allergen-free products - same principle as raw below ready-to-eat.
- Peanut and tree nut products in separate, clearly labeled containers. Never bulk with other allergens or shared scoops.
- If a bulk ingredient was scooped with a contaminated utensil, discard or reclassify it. Do not hope it was clean.

## 3.5 Peanut and Tree Nut Standard

> ANCHOR: No peanuts or tree nuts on buffet items. The default posture across PFS is peanut-free and tree-nut-free at the buffet.

**Permitted exceptions** - peanut and tree nut products are permitted on the line only when each unit is individually packaged, controlled at the unit level, and clearly labeled. Examples:

- Uncrustables and PB and Js (individually wrapped).
- Power bites and energy bars (individually packaged).
- PB on the acai station (controlled portion, dedicated utensil).
- Oatmeal bar toppings (individually portioned in small ramekins, not bulk).
- Other special cases approved by the Chef in writing.

> NOTE: A peanut or tree nut product appearing on the line outside these exceptions is removed before service. The Site Leader investigates how it got there before the next service.

# 04 Allergen-Free Meals and Stations

We do not offer them. Ever. This is a liability decision, not a capability decision.

> CRITICAL: We do not offer allergen-free meals. We do not offer allergen-free stations. Ever. We cannot guarantee an allergen-free plate. That is a liability we do not take on.

## Why we do not offer them

Allergen-free is a guarantee. A guarantee against contamination by a substance that can kill someone in minutes is not a guarantee KitchFix is positioned to make in a shared kitchen serving hundreds of meals a day. We do not have a dedicated allergen-free preparation area. We do not have dedicated equipment per allergen. We do not have a verification protocol that can confirm zero contamination at the plate.

Other operations may offer allergen-free meals. Their kitchens, their guarantees, their liability. KitchFix has decided differently - and the decision is final at the company level, not negotiable per site or per client.

## What we do instead

- Label accurately - every item carries an accurate Contains list.
- Prevent cross-contact - per Section 03 disciplines.
- Communicate transparently - when asked about an allergen, answer specifically.
- Let players decide - an informed player choosing to eat is exercising informed consent.

## Severe Allergy Accommodation

If a player with a severe allergy needs accommodation, the Chef handles it directly - one-on-one, coordinated through the dietitian, case by case. This is a leadership decision, not a line-level task or a standing program.

How accommodation works:

- The dietitian or medical staff communicates the specific allergy and severity to the Site Leader.
- The Chef reviews and decides whether KitchFix can safely accommodate. The Chef may decline if the request cannot be met without taking on liability that the standard prohibits.
- If the Chef decides to accommodate, the Chef defines the accommodation - specific dish, specific preparation, specific delivery channel - and personally supervises preparation.
- The accommodation is documented and reviewed for the duration of the season. It does not become a standing program.

> ANCHOR: Severe allergy accommodation is a Chef-level decision only. Sous Chefs and Hospitality Managers do not commit to accommodations on their own.

# 05 Site Leader Responsibilities

Allergen safety is led from the top of the site, not delegated. The Site Leader owns the standard at their account.

## 5.1 Certification

Every team member at the site holds ServSafe Allergen certification. The Site Leader verifies certification at hiring, tracks expirations in Rippling, and ensures renewals happen before they lapse.

## 5.2 Player Allergen Information

At the start of each season - and throughout as the roster changes - the Site Leader works with the Club to obtain current player allergen information.

- Source of truth - the Club's dietitian or medical staff provides the list. Not players directly, not assumptions.
- Updates - roster moves, trades, call-ups, rehab assignments - every change to the active roster triggers an allergen-info refresh.
- Distribution - shared with the kitchen team in writing. Not "told once at pre-shift." Written, accessible, current.

## 5.3 Daily Allergen Verification Review

Before every service, the Chef or Sous Chef walks the line with the menu and verifies labels are present, accurate, and visible. The walk is documented in the daily site log.

## 5.4 Pre-Shift Allergen Topic

Run a 2 to 3 minute allergen topic during pre-shift weekly. Rotate the topic: cross-contact disciplines, where this week's new menu items hide allergens, recent vendor substitutions, what to do if someone has a reaction.

## 5.5 Address Issues in the Moment

> ANCHOR: Address allergen issues in the moment. Not after service. Now. The standard you walk past is the standard you accept.

When a labeling miss, a cross-contact event, or a protocol violation surfaces during service, the Site Leader stops it then and there.

# 06 If Someone Has a Reaction

Six steps. Medical response is always Step 1. Documentation comes after.

> ANCHOR: Medical response is ALWAYS Step 1. Everything else comes after.

## 6.1 The Six Steps

### Step 1 - Get medical help immediately

Contact on-site medical staff. If the reaction is severe - difficulty breathing, swelling, loss of consciousness - call 911 even if site medical is responding. Stay with the person.

### Step 2 - Remove the food

Pull the suspected item and all utensils from the line. Preserve the packaging and any remaining product - do not throw it away.

### Step 3 - Stabilize

Clean the station, replace equipment, ensure safe service can continue. The kitchen does not shut down unless the Chef decides it should.

### Step 4 - Notify within 10 minutes

Notify the Client and KitchFix leadership within 10 minutes of the reaction being identified. Use the Severity 1 phone-tree per SOP-002 section 6.

### Step 5 - Document same day

File the incident report in the People Portal before end of shift. Capture root cause as best understood at the time. Preserve all remaining food and packaging in a labeled bag for follow-up review.

### Step 6 - Follow up within 24 hours

Reconnect with the Client within 24 hours. Share findings, corrective actions, and the retraining plan that addresses the gap.
