# 01 Purpose & How to Use

How we manage the client relationship - from standing up an account to renewing and growing it.

> ANCHOR: We don't have customers; we have partners whose clubhouses we work in every day. Accounts are kept the same way they're won - by delivering the promise and managing the relationship on purpose, not by accident.

This playbook defines how KitchFix manages client accounts - the roles, the SLA, the communication cadence, account health, change management, issue handling, and renewal. It applies to RDOs and Executive Chefs, who together own the client relationship at the regional and site level.

- The RDO owns the account - the relationship, the SLA, and account health.
- The EC owns the daily experience - the food, the service, and the day-to-day client touchpoint.
- This playbook is the how - the operating discipline lives in PB-010; the agreement lives in the SLA (PB-005).

# 02 The Account Relationship

We are a partner in the client's operation - trusted with their players, their space, and their reputation.

Every account is a relationship before it's a contract. The client trusts KitchFix to feed their athletes at the highest standard, in their house, every day. That trust is the asset - it's what makes an account renew, refer, and grow.

- The promise - <Fact id="brand_promise" /> - delivered consistently is what holds the relationship.
- The bar is consistency - one great day doesn't keep an account; the same great day every day does.

> NOTE: The client notices consistency more than peaks. A site that's excellent on the showcase day and ordinary the rest of the homestand has an account-health problem, even if no one's complained yet.

# 03 Account Roles

Clear ownership on both sides so nothing falls through the relationship.

## On the KitchFix side

- RDO - owns the account - the relationship, the SLA, account health, and renewals.
- General Manager/Executive Chef - owns the daily client experience and the on-site relationship; the client's day-to-day point of contact.
- Director of Culinary / Executive Chef / RD partnership - owns the menu and nutrition standard with the client's dietitian.

## On the client side

Know your account's key contacts - typically the team dietitian, the clubhouse or operations manager, and the decision-makers on scope and renewal. Map who owns what, and who to go to for which conversation.

> ANCHOR: Every account should be able to answer two questions instantly: who is our RDO, and who is our client's decision-maker. If either is fuzzy, the relationship is being run on autopilot.

# 04 The SLA

The SLA is the operating agreement - what we deliver, to what standard, under which model.

Each account runs on a Service Level Agreement that defines scope, standards, the financial model, and the client's specific requirements. It is the source of truth for what we owe the client and how the account operates.

- Client-driven vs KF-driven - some accounts run to the client's standards (their dietitian dictates); some run to KitchFix's lead. The SLA says which, and the floor-then-override logic captures it.
- The model - fee vs full-service - the SLA sets the financial terms (PB-009).
- Where it lives - the SLA system of record is PB-005 / the SLA templates (TPL-014); each account's SLA is built and maintained there.

> NOTE: When a question comes up - scope, a special request, who pays - the SLA answers it. If the SLA is silent or stale, that's the gap to close, not a judgment call to improvise.

# 05 Standing Up a New Account

A new account is won twice - in the sale, and in the first 30 days of execution.

1. Build the SLA - define scope, standards, model, and the client's requirements (PB-005, TPL-014).
2. Map the relationship - identify the client's key contacts and decision-makers; introduce the RDO and EC.
3. Stand up the site - staff, train, and set up the operation to PB-010; confirm food safety and compliance readiness.
4. Align the menu - build the menu with the client's dietitian and the Director of Culinary.
5. Set the cadence - establish the check-in and reporting rhythm from day one (Section 06).

> NOTE: The first homestand sets the client's expectation of what "normal" looks like. Over-resource the launch - it's cheaper than recovering a shaky first impression.

# 06 The Client Cadence

A relationship on a rhythm never surprises anyone - the client always knows where things stand.

| Cadence | What happens |
|---|---|
| Ongoing | Day-to-day GM/EC touchpoint - the client's read on the operation comes from the site, every day. |
| Regular check-in | Logged client check-ins (TPL-011) - short, consistent, owned by the GM/EC and RDO. |
| Periodic business review | RDO-led review of account health, performance, and what's next - the relationship-level conversation. |
| As needed | Proactive outreach on anything material - a change, an issue, a win - before the client hears it elsewhere. |

> ANCHOR: Communication is the cheapest retention tool there is. The accounts that churn are rarely the ones that were over-communicated with.

# 07 Account Health & Performance

Know whether an account is healthy before the client tells you it isn't.

Account health is the RDO's read on the relationship and the operation together - not just "are there complaints," but "is this account solid, at risk, or growing."

- Operational performance - is the site delivering the standard consistently (PB-010, self-audit)?
- Client satisfaction - is the client engaged, satisfied, and confident - by what they say and how they act?
- Financial health - is the account performing to model and budget (PB-009)?
- Renewal signal - is this account on a path to renew and grow, or showing risk?

> NOTE: A quiet account isn't automatically a healthy one. Silence can mean satisfied - or disengaged. The RDO's job is to know which.

# 08 Managing Change

Scope and menu change over a season - manage it through the right channel, never improvised on site.

- Menu changes - go through the client's dietitian and the Director of Culinary - never changed unilaterally on site (PB-010, PB-006).
- Scope changes - new services, added events, or expanded coverage are agreed and reflected in the SLA before they become standing expectations.
- Fun Money / event budgets - where an account funds events, manage to the agreed budget and document it.
- Document it - a change that isn't written into the SLA becomes a dispute later. Capture it.

> ANCHOR: "We've just always done it" is how scope creep becomes margin loss. Every standing change should trace to an agreement, not a habit.

# 09 Issues & Complaints

When something goes wrong, the recovery is the relationship test - own it fast.

1. Own it on site - the EC addresses the immediate issue and makes it right in the moment (PB-003 Service Recovery).
2. Communicate - tell the client what happened and what's being done - before they have to ask.
3. Escalate when needed - loop in the RDO for anything material, recurring, or relationship-level.
4. Fix the cause - resolve the root cause so it doesn't repeat; note the pattern.

> CRITICAL: A handled problem can strengthen an account; a hidden or repeated one ends it. Never let the client discover an issue we knew about and didn't surface.

# 10 In the Client's House

Everything we do happens in the client's space, around their players - conduct is part of account management.

- Respect the space, the players, and the client's staff - how we carry ourselves is the brand (POL-014).
- Player health, dietary, and personal information is strictly confidential - never shared or discussed (POL-014).
- Appearance and conduct meet standard on site, every shift (POL-002).

> NOTE: Discretion is account management. A breach of player confidentiality isn't an HR issue - it's an account-ending event. Treat it that way.

# 11 Renewals & Growth

The best new business is a renewed, growing account - earn it all season, not at renewal time.

- Renewals are earned daily - the renewal conversation is shaped by every homestand, not by the pitch at the end.
- Know the timeline - the RDO tracks each account's renewal window and starts the conversation early, from a position of demonstrated performance.
- Grow within the account - added services, events, or scope - surfaced when the relationship and the performance support it.
- References & expansion - a great account is the credibility behind the next one - in MLB, MiLB, and into new leagues.

> ANCHOR: Retention is the strategy. KitchFix grows by keeping accounts excellent and letting that excellence open the next door.
